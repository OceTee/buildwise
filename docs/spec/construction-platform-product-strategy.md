# Construction planning and marketplace platform

## Evidence-backed product strategy and MVP specification

**Prepared:** 12 November 2026  
**Status:** Product hypothesis for validation; geography remains undecided

## 1. Executive recommendation

Build a **managed preconstruction and procurement service** for one narrow customer segment and one launch geography. Help a client turn a sufficiently complete, professionally designed small building project into a reviewed scope and estimate, compare supplier and trade quotes, then track the first procurement packages and site milestones.

Do not initially promise an instant, construction-ready bill of quantities (BoQ) from any arbitrary drawing, universal contractor coverage, or full end-to-end construction management. Those promises depend on drawing quality, structural and services information, local price data, verified providers, local permitting, and dispute operations. The references already show competitors covering pieces of the idea—including plan takeoff, estimates, quote requests, material sourcing, artisan discovery, logistics, and project records.

The defensible initial product is the **trusted handoff between a reviewed scope and local execution**: show what is known, what is assumed, who reviewed it, when prices were checked, what is excluded, and who is accountable for each next step.

### Initial decision

The referenced conversation names no country, city, currency, regulatory environment, or user location. **Nigeria is not supported by the available context.** Treat it as one candidate market only. Before product-market-fit work, choose a single city/region after customer interviews and supply-side validation. If Nigeria is chosen, also choose one state and one metro area; building approvals and operating conditions vary by state and local authority.

### Recommended MVP wedge

- **Buyer:** owner-builder or small developer who already has an architect’s concept/design for a simple, low-rise residential project.
- **Project:** one-storey or straightforward two-storey residential shell-to-finish project, deliberately excluding complex commercial, high-rise, major renovation, and specialist industrial work at launch.
- **Geography:** one metro and a bounded delivery radius; select only after research.
- **Core job:** make a reviewed, traceable estimate and obtain comparable quotes for a small number of material packages and trades.
- **Human review:** a qualified estimator/quantity surveyor (QS) validates quantities, assumptions, omissions and price basis before an estimate is labelled reviewed or used for tender/procurement.
- **MVP marketplace:** curated providers and request-for-quotation (RFQ) workflow. Start with manual sourcing/operations; do not rely on an empty open marketplace.

### Construction estimating workflow

A credible estimate progresses through controlled steps:

1. **Define the brief:** use, location, floor count, size, quality level, services, site conditions, budget, schedule, and what is included/excluded.
2. **Establish design maturity:** collect revision-controlled architectural, structural, mechanical/electrical/plumbing (MEP) drawings, specifications, schedules and site information. Identify missing or conflicting documents.
3. **Calibrate and measure:** verify units and drawing scale; measure lengths, areas, volumes and counts; cross-check schedules and related sheets; record markups and measurement basis.
4. **Build scope and quantities:** classify work by an appropriate local measurement convention; state descriptions, units, quantity derivation, waste/allowances and exclusions; use assemblies only where assumptions are disclosed.
5. **Price:** separate materials, labour, plant/equipment, delivery, preliminaries, taxes/fees where applicable, overhead/profit and contingency. Record source, location, date, validity and quote terms for each rate.
6. **Review and issue:** independent review of scope, arithmetic, drawing coordination, market rates, risk and design completeness; issue a dated estimate with confidence/maturity label and change control.
7. **Tender/procure:** issue the same scope and quantities to providers; compare like-for-like compliant quotes; clarify substitutions, delivery, payment terms, lead time, warranty and exclusions; document award.
8. **Track delivery and execution:** approve purchase orders, acknowledge delivery, document inspection/shortages, measure work, approve variations, compare committed/actual cost with budget and close out.

For Nigeria, an NIQS technical report describes a BoQ as itemized work measured from drawings and specifications using a method of measurement, and identifies tendering, contract award, interim valuation, variation and final account work across the QS lifecycle. It references BESMM4(R) for Nigerian building works. See the [NIQS technical report](https://register.niqs.org.ng/mature/otherdoc/P02012O_623.pdf) and [NIQS examination syllabus](https://niqs.org.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/NIQS-PROFESSIONAL-EXAMINATION-MODIFIED-SYLLABUS-2021.pdf). A BoQ is not just a material shopping list: it is a structured scope and measurement basis.

### Why local price and market operations matter

For Nigeria, World Bank sources report materials can account for a large share of construction cost: one country private-sector diagnostic states 35–80%, and a housing-finance paper gives 40–70% depending on project type/scale/quality. These older estimates differ, but both support the strategic importance of materials and logistics. They are **not current cost benchmarks**. A 2025 Nigeria building-material price dataset describes price movement across materials and years; local platform prices need supplier confirmation and freshness metadata, not a national static price list. See the [World Bank diagnostic](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/791981613750124789/pdf/Country-Private-Sector-Diagnostic-Creating-Markets-in-Nigeria-Crowding-in-the-Private-Sector-Nigeria-s-Path-to-Faster-Job-Creation-and-Structural-Transformation.pdf), [World Bank housing-finance report](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/102491481528326920/pdf/110897-WP-P131973-PUBLIC-HousingFinanceNigeriaweb.pdf), and [2025 price dataset](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340925007978).

### Regulatory and professional boundaries

If Nigeria is selected, permitting and qualified-professional requirements must be mapped for the launch state and local government. For example, [Niger State’s published guidance](https://nsudb.nigerstate.gov.ng/guidelines-for-application-and-obtaining-building/) says a development permit is required and plans should be prepared, stamped and signed by qualified professionals. [Lagos building-control guidance](https://lasbca.lagos.gov.ng/faq/) describes inspections, use of relevant professionals and compliance with permit drawings. These are jurisdiction-specific examples, not a complete or universal legal interpretation. The platform should facilitate referral and document tracking; it should not imply that its estimate is a permit, design certification, structural review or professional sign-off.

## 3. Product goal, users and value proposition

### Product goal

Reduce the time, uncertainty and avoidable disputes between a building brief/design and a responsibly scoped, priced, sourced and monitored project—while making uncertainty and professional responsibility visible.

### Primary personas

- **Owner-builder:** wants an understandable budget, guidance on what documents are missing, trustworthy trades and supplier options, and visibility into spending and progress.
- **Small developer / business owner:** needs repeatable estimates, quote comparisons, delivery coordination and evidence for each decision.
- **QS / estimator:** needs structured drawings, measurement traceability, reusable rate/assembly data, review tools and clear revision history.
- **Supplier:** needs complete, comparable RFQs with exact specifications, quantity, delivery location/time and a simple quote/order workflow.
- **Trade contractor:** needs qualified leads with defined scope, site conditions, schedule, payment milestones and change records.
- **Platform operations / reviewer:** verifies identity and credentials, assists with scope quality, monitors quotes and orders, handles incidents and resolves disputes.

### Positioning

“A guided, professionally reviewed route from your building plans to comparable local quotes and controlled project delivery.” Avoid “AI builds your house,” “guaranteed cost,” “all contractors vetted” or “complete BOQ from any plan” until supported by measured performance and actual coverage.

## 4. MVP scope

### In scope

1. **Guided project intake**
   - Project type, address/area, site status, floors, dimensions, desired finish band, budget range, start window and delivery constraints.
   - Upload PDF drawings and supporting files; capture revision/date and professional author where known.
   - Checklist for minimum information; mark missing sheets and uncertain assumptions.
2. **Two estimate entry paths**
   - Early concept: rules-based area/typology range, prominently labelled *indicative*, with broad exclusions and no procurement quantities.
   - Plan-based: human QS/estimator performs or validates takeoff and BoQ-style scope; digitally record quantity derivation, source drawing/sheet/revision and markup.
3. **Estimate review and versioning**
   - Work sections and line items, quantity/unit, material/labour/plant split where practical, rate source/date/location, assumptions, inclusions, exclusions and contingency.
   - Review status: draft → information required → professionally reviewed → client accepted → superseded.
   - Clear validity date and notices when rates expire or design changes.
4. **Supplier RFQs for a limited set of packages**
   - User selects lines/packages and requests itemized quote from matched suppliers.
   - Normalize unit, brand/specification, substitutions, stock, taxes, delivery and quote expiry; compare and ask clarifying questions.
   - Admin can source quotes where marketplace density is low.
5. **Trade provider discovery and shortlisting**
   - Launch with a small number of trades that customer research validates; provider profile, service radius, credentials/identity checks, references, insurance where applicable, availability, response time and platform status.
   - Invite-only or operations-approved providers first; no unmoderated self-serve “verified” badge.
6. **Simple project coordination**
   - Milestones, owner, target dates, dependencies, status, evidence photos, notes and client approvals.
   - Purchase/order record, delivery confirmation, discrepancy report, payment milestone record, variation request and closeout summary.
7. **Admin console**
   - Provider onboarding and re-check dates, quote monitoring, estimate assignment/review, incident and dispute queue, refunds/chargebacks records, and audit log.

### Explicitly out of scope for MVP

- Construction-ready BoQ automatically generated from arbitrary PDF, photo, sketch, CAD or prose without qualified review.
- Structural design, engineering calculations, architectural design or building permit approval.
- National rollout, all building types, major commercial/high-rise projects, or every trade and supplier category.
- Guaranteed fixed-price project delivery or platform warranty for workmanship.
- Holding construction funds/escrow before legal, payments, licensing, safeguarding and dispute policies are reviewed for the chosen market.
- Lending, insurance underwriting, land title verification, full ERP/accounting, BIM authoring, 3D visualization, or autonomous scheduling.

## 5. User journeys

### A. Homeowner from brief to estimate

1. Create project, choose “early budget” or “I have drawings,” and provide city/site and intended building use.
2. Complete a plain-language intake and upload documents; app checks file readability and asks for missing critical sheets.
3. App returns either (a) a broad indicative range with explicit assumptions, or (b) an estimate-review offer and expected turnaround.
4. Estimator reviews measurements and gaps, asks clarification questions, and issues a versioned reviewed estimate.
5. Owner sees cost by work section, main cost drivers, exclusions, rate age, confidence/maturity and downloadable/shareable report.
6. Owner accepts a version and selects procurement packages/trades for quotes.

### B. QS / estimator review

1. Open assigned project with drawing register and revision history.
2. Confirm scale and units; inspect architectural/structural/services information; flag missing or conflicting details.
3. Create takeoff items with sheet/revision reference, marked-up evidence, unit, formula and quantity; add assumptions and waste factors explicitly.
4. Price from a location/date-aware rate library or verified supplier quote; separate quote from benchmark rate.
5. Run completeness checks, submit for second-person review where thresholds or risk rules require it, and issue signed reviewer identity/qualification record.
6. Later design/rate changes create a new version and compare quantity, price and scope deltas.

### C. Supplier quote and order

1. Supplier receives RFQ with exact line specifications, units, quantities, delivery area, requested delivery date and quote deadline.
2. Supplier quotes line by line, including brand/grade, quantity basis, unit price, availability, delivery, taxes, substitutions, expiry and payment terms.
3. Owner or procurement operator compares normalized quotes and asks questions; system identifies non-compliant substitutions and missing fields.
4. Owner awards quote, records payment outside the platform initially or through a compliant payment partner if later enabled, and receives order confirmation.
5. Delivery is confirmed with timestamp/photo/recipient and discrepancy report; resolution is tracked against order terms.

### D. Trade hiring and site coordination

1. Owner chooses a defined work package; project scope and site visit conditions are shown to matched providers.
2. Providers submit price, scope inclusions/exclusions, materials responsibility, schedule, warranty/defects terms and milestones.
3. Owner compares compliant offers and checks credentials/references; contract is agreed and stored.
4. Milestones show dependencies and evidence; changes require priced variation and owner approval before work proceeds, except a documented safety emergency.
5. Completion evidence, snag/defect list, final measurement/payment and provider review are recorded.

## 6. Estimation and confidence model

### Do not collapse these outputs into one “AI estimate”

| Output | Allowed basis | Label shown to user | Procurement allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early budget range | Typology, floor area, quality band, location and dated benchmark rates | Indicative range; assumptions and exclusions; not a BoQ | No, unless manually converted/reviewed |
| Assisted takeoff draft | Machine-detected geometry/counts or user-entered measurements from identified drawing pages | Draft; quantities require review; list uncertain/low-confidence items | No |
| Reviewed estimate / BoQ-style scope | Measured quantities and documented scope, checked by an appropriately qualified professional; rate basis dated | Reviewed by named reviewer; design maturity and validity date; exclusions | Yes, for the stated package and version, with quote confirmation |
| Supplier quote | Supplier’s specific price, stock, delivery terms and validity | Supplier-confirmed; valid until stated date; terms attached | Yes, subject to award and contract/order terms |

### Estimation engine principles

- Begin with deterministic quantity formulas and professional takeoff; use AI for document classification, sheet indexing, symbol suggestions, duplicate/missing-sheet detection, extraction of schedules and draft measurements only.
- Preserve provenance for every measured quantity: source file checksum, revision, page/sheet, scale/calibration, measurement geometry, formula, author, edits, and reviewer.
- Never infer structural member sizes, reinforcement, foundations, MEP routes/capacities, ground conditions, code compliance or hidden work from architectural plans alone.
- Require human confirmation for drawing scale, units, material/specification, assembly assumptions, waste factors, scope inclusions and critical quantities.
- Separate quantities from rates. Price tables carry location, supplier/source, observation timestamp, unit, specification, availability, delivery, taxes and expiration/confidence.
- Show both a base estimate and explicit uncertainty/contingency, avoiding false precision. Track estimate versus awarded quotes and actuals to calibrate ranges.
- Automated validation: unit consistency, arithmetic, duplicate/omitted line checks, drawing revision mismatch, stale prices, abnormal rate/quantity deltas, and required approval gates.

### Initial data strategy

Start with a manually governed catalog for a narrow typology and launch region. Store material taxonomy, local units and conversion rules, specification/grade, assemblies and approved source. Collect dated quote observations from participating suppliers and QS/estimators. Display “last confirmed” and reject expired prices from a live quote comparison. Do not scrape or blend incompatible units/specifications without review. Offer imports/exports to spreadsheets because many firms already work there.

## 7. Marketplace model and operations

### Liquidity strategy

1. Choose one compact service area and recruit enough qualified suppliers and providers in a few high-value categories before customer launch.
2. Use request-for-quote and concierge matching; ensure each request receives a minimum number of comparable responses or explain the shortage and offer manual outreach.
3. Focus on reliable response and delivery before broad category count. Track quote response, fill rate, delivery performance, disputes and repeat use by category.
4. Avoid publishing unverified listings or representing a profile as vetted when only identity has been checked.

### Provider checks

- Business/legal name, beneficial contact, verified phone/email, government ID and business address (store minimum necessary data securely).
- Applicable professional registration and license checked with issuing body, scope and expiry; credentials do not imply quality guarantee.
- Trade capability, service radius, references and completed work examples; site visit/skills check for selected trade categories.
- Supplier business verification, product specification/brand, batch/quality documentation where applicable, stock confirmation, return policy and delivery capacity.
- Insurance and safety documentation where legally or commercially relevant; record expiration and re-check status.
- Disclose verification level and date: “identity checked,” “credential checked,” “references checked,” and “performance history,” rather than a single ambiguous badge.

### Trust, payments and disputes

- Written quote, scope, exclusions, schedule, variation rules, delivery evidence and acceptance criteria for each award.
- Do not call funds “escrow” unless the business and payment arrangement legally qualify and safeguards are actually implemented. In MVP, record payment evidence or use a regulated payment partner without custody of funds.
- Separate supplier order disputes (wrong item, shortage, late delivery, quality) from workmanship disputes (scope, defect, safety, delay); provide evidence capture, response deadlines and escalation to an independent reviewer.
- Never imply platform ratings certify structural safety. Allow right of reply and appeal; detect coercive review behavior and fraud.
- Publish refund, cancellation, dispute and provider-removal policies; establish incident response and emergency safety escalation.

### Revenue hypotheses to test

- Paid professional estimate/review fee paid by the client, with transparent scope and fixed review level.
- Supplier subscription or qualified lead fee after proving lead quality and clear disclosure.
- Procurement transaction fee or disclosed referral commission only after tracking order completion and addressing conflicts of interest.
- Contractor SaaS tools for estimates, records or leads.

Avoid taking undisclosed commissions that distort QS recommendations. Keep “professional recommendation” distinct from sponsored listing and price ranking. Test willingness to pay and local unit economics before choosing the model.

## 8. Technical architecture

### MVP architecture (implementation-neutral)

- **Client apps:** responsive web app first, optimized for low-bandwidth mobile use; consider native Android after evidence of field need. Client, provider, reviewer and operations roles share project-level permissions.
- **Core API:** modular monolith initially, with bounded modules for identity/access, projects/files, estimation, catalog/rates, RFQs/quotes, orders, milestones, messaging/notifications, reviews/disputes and admin.
- **Relational database:** transactional project, estimate versions, line items, providers, RFQs, offers, orders, milestones, consent and audit events. Enforce tenant/project access and immutable version references.
- **Object storage:** private plan files, credentials and site evidence; encryption, short-lived signed URLs, malware scanning, retention/deletion rules and access logs.
- **Document processing:** asynchronous queue for PDF validation, page previews, OCR/indexing and optional AI extraction. Store model/version, source page, confidence and reviewer corrections. Processing failure must not block manual upload workflow.
- **Search/matching:** location radius, trade/material category, credential status, availability, quote responsiveness and service capability. Ranking logic transparent and not pay-to-win by default.
- **Notifications:** email/SMS/WhatsApp integrations only with explicit consent and local vendor/legal review; preserve an in-app record as system of record.
- **Payments:** initially no platform-held construction funds; later integrate locally suitable licensed payment service with split-payment, refund and reconciliation design after counsel review.
- **Observability/security:** least privilege, MFA for operations, rate limiting, encrypted backups, audit trail, secrets management, incident logging, monitoring for upload abuse and account takeover, recovery drills.

### Core data entities

`User`, `Organization`, `Project`, `ProjectRole`, `Site`, `Document`, `DrawingRevision`, `EstimateVersion`, `WorkSection`, `EstimateLine`, `TakeoffMeasurement`, `RateObservation`, `Assumption`, `ReviewDecision`, `Provider`, `Credential`, `CatalogItem`, `RFQ`, `Quote`, `Award`, `PurchaseOrder`, `Delivery`, `TradePackage`, `Milestone`, `Variation`, `Evidence`, `PaymentRecord`, `Dispute`, `Consent`, `AuditEvent`.

### Critical architecture properties

- Estimate line and quote must retain exact project/design revision; changes create new versions, never silently mutate issued numbers.
- Track unit/specification identity and conversions; prevent “bag,” “ton,” “piece,” area and volume mismatches from being silently compared.
- Access control at project and document level; providers see only awarded/invited scope and necessary site details.
- Data export/delete and retention controls; do not train shared models on private architectural drawings without clear consent and legal basis.
- For Nigeria, assess obligations under the Nigeria Data Protection Act and current NDPC guidance, including controller/processor roles, lawful basis, security, data-subject rights, breach handling, and any registration/compliance obligations. The [NDPC](https://ndpc.gov.ng/) is the regulator; obtain local counsel/compliance advice for the selected model.

## 9. Risks and controls

| Risk | Why it matters | MVP control / launch gate |
|---|---|---|
| False precision from incomplete plans or AI | Under-ordering/over-ordering, unsafe work, cost overruns and liability | Separate indicative, draft and reviewed output; no construction-ready plan extraction without qualified review; measure error on benchmark plans before any automation claim. |
| Design coordination gaps | Architecture alone may omit structural/MEP/site details | Required document checklist; show exclusions; reject or label estimate incomplete when critical inputs missing. |
| Stale or incomparable prices | Inflation, FX, geography, delivery and units materially affect totals | Dated, location-specific observations; supplier-confirmed quotes and expiries; comparable specification and delivered price. |
| Marketplace cold start | Users see few/no viable providers; providers receive poor leads | One area and few categories, concierge matching, response SLAs, provider interviews and category-level liquidity gate. |
| Fraud, credential misrepresentation or low workmanship | Financial loss and physical safety harm | Tiered checks, issuer verification, references, evidence, clear badge semantics, rechecks, incident and suspension process. |
| Disputes/variations | Scope gaps create conflict and cost blowouts | Written scope, exclusions, change orders, milestone evidence, communication record, escalation and independent review. |
| Payment custody / financial regulation | Holding funds adds licensing, safeguarding and reconciliation requirements | No custody in MVP; local counsel and payments partner before enabling protected funds. |
| Construction injury or building failure | High-severity physical harm | Exclude design/engineering certification; require licensed professionals and permits; safety escalation; appropriate insurance and contractual boundaries. |
| Privacy of plans and identity documents | Plans reveal home layouts; IDs/credentials are sensitive personal data | Private encrypted storage, minimization, scoped access, retention limits, access log, consent and deletion controls. |
| Local regulatory variation | Permit/professional rules are jurisdiction-specific | Launch geography registry; legal review; link official authorities; do not market permit compliance as automated. |
| Conflict of interest in recommendations | Commission can bias professional estimate or supplier choice | Disclose paid placements/fees; separate QS review from sales ranking; preserve quote comparison audit. |

## 10. Success measures and experiments

### North-star outcome

**Projects that progress from accepted, reviewed scope to a successfully completed first procurement/trade milestone with no unresolved platform-attributable dispute.** This is more meaningful than uploads, generated estimates or leads alone.

### MVP metrics

- Intake completion and share of projects with adequate design information.
- Turnaround time and reviewer hours per estimate; estimate revision rate and material quantity variance against reviewed benchmark/actual quantities.
- Difference between estimate rate basis, awarded quote and final paid price, explained by scope/date/location.
- RFQ response rate, qualified quotes per request, time to first compliant quote, award rate, supplier fill and on-time/in-full delivery.
- Provider credential pass/recheck rate, cancellation/no-show, complaint and dispute rate, resolution time and repeat work.
- Project milestone completion, variation value, cost-to-date variance and user-reported confidence.
- Gross margin per estimate/review and per completed procurement; cost of concierge operations and customer acquisition.

### First 6–8 week validation plan

1. **Geography choice:** interview 12–15 prospective buyers and 10–15 providers in each of at most two candidate metros; select one based on urgent pain, reachable supply and operator access—not population alone.
2. **Workflow observation:** shadow 5–8 real estimates from brief/drawings through supplier quote; collect anonymized artifacts with permission. Document spreadsheet, WhatsApp, paper and phone handoffs.
3. **Estimate benchmark:** ask 3–5 qualified QS/estimators to independently review a small set of past plans/BoQs; document disagreement, missing information, time and rate refresh needs.
4. **Competitor mystery shop:** request the same sample service/quote from BMM, PAPAPA NOW and other active local options; test response times, pricing, provider checks, quote comparability, delivery and dispute handling.
5. **Concierge pilot:** 5–10 active projects, one project type, manual estimate review and RFQ matching; do not automate beyond measured/repetitive steps.
6. **Decision gate:** continue only if buyers will pay for review/coordination, qualified providers respond reliably, estimates can be produced at viable cost, and users understand uncertainty and professional boundaries.

Suggested initial thresholds to validate, not promises: at least 3 comparable supplier responses for common packages within 72 hours; 80% of RFQs receive one compliant response; reviewed estimate turnaround under 5 business days after complete inputs; rate observations refreshed within a clearly stated period appropriate to volatility; no unresolved severe safety/fraud incidents. Set final thresholds after pilot data.

## 11. Phased roadmap

### Phase 0 — Discovery and service blueprint (weeks 0–8)

- Select city and project segment; interview, observe jobs, mystery shop competitors.
- Recruit QS lead and provider operations lead; define professional review and incident policies.
- Create manual rate catalog and first validated estimate templates; define provider tiers and quote normalization.
- Pilot concierge estimates and RFQs using simple secure forms/workspace. Decide whether product economics support software build.

### Phase 1 — MVP (approximately months 2–5 after gate)

- Guided intake, private file/drawing register, estimate versioning and reviewer workflow.
- Manual/assisted takeoff capture with marked-up evidence and downloadable estimate.
- Supplier/trade profiles, RFQ distribution, normalized quotes, messaging and award record.
- Basic milestones, delivery evidence, variation records and operations console.
- Privacy/security baseline, audit trail, incident/dispute handling and customer support.

### Phase 2 — Repeatability (months 5–9)

- Localized catalog and supplier price observations; quote comparison and stale-price alerts.
- Reusable templates/assemblies for proven project types; spreadsheet import/export.
- Provider self-service with staged verification and operational quality scores.
- Purchase orders, delivery tracking, mobile-friendly field updates and cost-to-complete.
- Test paid estimate tiers, supplier subscription and disclosed transaction fees.

### Phase 3 — Carefully bounded automation (months 9–15)

- PDF page classification, revision comparison, schedule extraction and symbol/measurement suggestions.
- Benchmark automated suggestions against qualified takeoffs; display confidence and require review for measured outputs.
- Add geographies only when provider liquidity, price coverage, legal mapping and operations capacity meet gates.
- Integrate payment partner only after legal, consumer protection, safeguarding, reconciliation and dispute design.

### Phase 4 — Broader project coordination (after repeatable execution)

- Trade package sequencing, schedule dependencies, approvals and progress-to-cost analytics.
- Additional commercial categories and complex buildings only with specialist workflows and professional review.
- Potential design/BIM and permitting integrations where officially supported and safe.

## 12. Key product decisions still to resolve

1. **Launch geography:** country, city/metro, and operating radius (currently unknown; Nigeria is only a candidate).
2. **First paying customer:** owner-builder, small developer, architect/QS practice, supplier, or contractor. Recommendation: owner-builder pays for review; providers pay only after demonstrable lead value.
3. **Initial project type:** choose one simple low-rise typology based on real local demand and available drawings.
4. **Professional model:** employ/contract qualified reviewers, partner with independent QS firms, or provide software used by the customer’s own QS.
5. **Payment model:** information/quote workflow only at first, or compliant payment partner at launch.
6. **Provider verification promise:** define exact checks and how the user sees them.
7. **Price data ownership:** supplier-confirmed updates, human price desk, licensed datasets, or a combination.
