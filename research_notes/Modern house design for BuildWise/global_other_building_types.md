# Good contemporary design for non-house building types (global): apartments, terraces, offices, retail and mixed-use, hotels, schools, clinics, worship and event halls

Scope note: these notes favour primary guidance documents (NSW Apartment Design Guide, GLA Housing Design Standards LPG 2023, UK DfE baseline school designs, Singapore URA handbooks) and architects' or award bodies' project pages. The ADG and GLA figures were read from the full PDF text. Exemplar-project facts mostly come from search-result summaries of Dezeen, MoMA, AKDN, ArchDaily and similar pages, so they are secondary unless stated otherwise. Where a figure rests on a single secondary source, it is flagged.

## 1. Apartment buildings and terraces: plan types, corridors, dual aspect, balconies, entrances, facades and exemplars

### Takeaway
Current guidance converges on a few rules. Plans are shallow, 12–18 m glass to glass. No more than 8 flats share a core on one floor. Most flats should be dual aspect or cross-ventilated, which in practice means short corridors or open access decks instead of long double-loaded corridors. Every flat gets a usable balcony at least 1.5–2.4 m deep, opening off the living room. The entrance is visible, glazed and daylit, and the facade has a defined base, middle and top, with articulation from balconies, deep reveals and grouped floors. Award-winning housing (Peter Barber, Lacaton & Vassal, Elemental, Pinnacle@Duxton) gets its character from street-based entrances, generous private outdoor space and shared elevated or courtyard gardens, not from extra ornament.

### Cited Findings

**Plan depth and building envelope**
- The NSW Apartment Design Guide (ADG) recommends "a range of appropriate maximum apartment depths of 12-18m from glass line to glass line" when setting building envelopes. It suggests buildings facing north–south should be narrower than east–west ones, and says greater depths need "significant building articulation and increased perimeter wall length". For mixed-use buildings, the depth steps from a deeper retail podium to a narrower residential block above. — [NSW ADG Part 2, 2E Building depth](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-2-developing-the-controls.pdf)
- The ADG says a cross-over or cross-through apartment's overall depth should not exceed 18 m, measured glass line to glass line (4B-3 design criterion 2). — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- ADG 4D-2 limits habitable room depth to 2.5 × the ceiling height, with a maximum of 8 m from a window in open-plan living/dining/kitchen layouts. Cross-over or cross-through apartments must be at least 4 m wide internally "to avoid deep narrow apartment layouts". — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- ADG 4D-1 says every habitable room needs a window in an external wall with glass of at least 10% of the room's floor area, and "daylight and air may not be borrowed from other rooms". The guidance adds that "a window should be visible from any point in a habitable room" and that all living areas and bedrooms should be on the external face of the building. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)

**Building separation (spacing between blocks)**
- ADG 2F gives minimum separations:
  - Up to 4 storeys (about 12 m high): 12 m between habitable rooms or balconies, 9 m between habitable and non-habitable rooms, 6 m between non-habitable rooms.
  - 5–8 storeys (about 25 m): 18 m, 12 m and 9 m.
  - 9 storeys and above: 24 m, 18 m and 12 m.
  - Add 3 m to the setback at a zone boundary with lower-density housing.
  - No separation is needed where buildings share blank party walls, as on main streets or podiums.
  — [NSW ADG Part 2](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-2-developing-the-controls.pdf)

**Cores, corridors, decks**
- ADG 4F-1: at most **8 apartments off a circulation core on a single level**. In buildings of 10 storeys or more, at most **40 apartments share a single lift**. Where 8 cannot be achieved, the cap is 12 per level, with compensating amenity such as daylit corridors, seating and generous widths. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- The ADG also says that **corridors longer than 12 m from the lift core "should be articulated"**, for example with a series of foyers with windows and seating, wider areas at apartment doors and varied ceiling heights. All common circulation above ground should get daylight and natural ventilation, with windows next to the stair or lift core or at corridor ends. Primary living room or bedroom windows should not open directly onto common circulation. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- London Housing Design Standards LPG (2023):
  - B2.5: the number of homes accessed by a core "should not exceed eight per floor". Exceptions must be mitigated with 1,800 mm corridors, homes on both sides of the core and intermediate doors that create sub-clusters.
  - B2.1: corridors at least 1,500 mm wide.
  - B2.2: double-banked corridors kept short, with daylight and natural ventilation.
  - B2.3 (best practice): access galleries or decks "that facilitate dual aspect homes as an alternative to internal corridors", though decks are "unlikely to be appropriate in the upper floors of tall buildings".
  — [GLA Housing Design Standards LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- The LPG's explanatory text says developments "should therefore avoid long narrow corridors. Covered outside decks are a healthier, safer and more convivial solution, and allow dwellings to be dual aspect." — [GLA LPG 2023, para 3.1.4](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- A search summary reports that a later GLA document has a section on "Dual aspect and dwellings per core" that replaces some 2023 standards. Its contents were not retrieved. — [GLA (london.gov.uk search result)](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/112374/download)

**Dual aspect and cross-ventilation**
- London LPG C4.1: "New homes should be dual aspect unless exceptional circumstances make this impractical or undesirable." Single-aspect homes, where allowed, are limited to one or two bedspaces, must not face north, and must show they ventilate and do not overheat. — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- The LPG Appendix 3 definition of dual aspect:
  - Opening windows on two external walls, opposite or adjacent. One aspect may face an access deck or courtyard.
  - Bay windows, stepped frontages, shallow recesses or projecting facades do **not** count.
  - On adjacent walls, the windows must sit at least halfway down the depth of the dwelling.
  - The angle between the two aspects must be no more than 135°.
  — [GLA LPG 2023, Appendix 3](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- ADG 4B-3: at least **60% of apartments naturally cross-ventilated** in the first nine storeys. Apartments at 10 storeys and above count as cross-ventilated if their balconies allow adequate ventilation. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf); [NSW Better Apartment Design FAQ](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/better-apartment-design-faq.pdf)
- ADG 4A-1 sets two sunlight targets. At least 70% of apartments get 2 hours of direct midwinter sun (9am–3pm) to the living room and private open space in Sydney, Newcastle and Wollongong, or 3 hours elsewhere. At most 15% get no direct midwinter sun. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)

**Balconies and private outdoor space**
- ADG 4E-1 primary balcony minimums:
  - Studio: 4 m².
  - 1-bed: 8 m², 2 m deep.
  - 2-bed: 10 m², 2 m deep.
  - 3+ bed: 12 m², 2.4 m deep.
  - Only parts at least 1 m deep count towards the area.
  - Ground-floor or podium apartments get a private open space of at least 15 m² and 3 m deep instead.
  The guidance says balconies "articulate the building facade". — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- London LPG C10.1: at least 5 m² of step-free private outside space for one or two bedspaces, plus 1 m² per extra bedspace, with a minimum depth and width of 1.5 m. Inset balconies must be shallower than their open face is wide.
- London LPG C10.2 (best practice): depth and width of at least 2.5 m.
- London LPG C10.3: the balcony opens off the main sitting area or kitchen/dining room, ideally with adjustable sliding screens or awnings for shade and privacy.
- London LPG C10.4: glazed, ventilated winter gardens suit noisy or windy high-level locations.
  — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- Singapore URA caps the bonus GFA for balconies and private enclosed spaces at **7% of residential GFA**, down from 10% for applications from 17 January 2019. Balcony screens (fins, louvres) are allowed only if they can be drawn fully open or retracted, and they must be approved with the development application. — [URA balconies handbook](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/residential/flats-condominiums/balconies-pes-prt/); [URA circular DC18-07](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/archived-circulars/dc18-07/)

**Entrances, lobbies, ground floors**
- London LPG:
  - B1.1: private and communal entrances visible and clearly identifiable.
  - B1.2: ground-floor flats and maisonettes have their own front door.
  - B1.4: the entrance lobby is "safe, welcoming, durable, well-lit and at least partially glazed", with wayfinding signage.
  - B1.5: lifts and stairs within sight of the entrance, and the stairs "prominent and attractive".
  - C4.4: avoid bedrooms and bathrooms on street-facing ground-floor facades.
  — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- The ADG says ground-floor apartments should address the public domain, with street entrances maximising frontage activity (4L). It also says building entries should be "clearly defined" (4M-2). — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)

**Facade composition**
- ADG 4M-1 lists design solutions for front facades:
  - "a composition of varied building elements".
  - "a defined base, middle and top of buildings".
  - "revealing and concealing certain elements".
  - Changes in texture, material, detail and colour.
  - "well composed horizontal and vertical elements".
  - "variation in floor heights to enhance the human scale".
  - "grouping of floors or elements such as balconies and windows on taller buildings".
  - Relating to the datum lines of neighbouring buildings (parapets, cornices, awnings, colonnade heights).
  - Shadow from "building articulation, balconies and deeper window reveals".
  — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- ADG 4M-2 adds three points. Important corners get "visual prominence through a change in articulation, materials or colour, roof expression or changes in height". The apartment layout should be expressed outside through party walls and floor slabs. A mix of apartment types reflected in the facade composition adds interest (fig. 4K.2). — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)

**Unit sizes, rooms and ceilings**
- ADG minimum internal areas are 35 m² (studio), 50 m² (1-bed), 70 m² (2-bed) and 90 m² (3-bed). Each extra bathroom adds 5 m² and each bedroom beyond three adds 12 m². Master bedrooms are at least 10 m² and other bedrooms 9 m², each with a minimum dimension of 3 m. Living rooms are at least 3.6 m wide in studios and 1-beds, and 4 m in 2–3-beds. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- ADG 4C minimum ceiling heights are 2.7 m for habitable rooms and 2.4 m for non-habitable rooms. In mixed-use areas, ground and first floor are 3.3 m to allow future change of use. The guidance recommends 3.3 m for cafés and restaurants. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)
- The ADG height-setting method adds 0.4 m per floor to the floor-to-ceiling height for structure, services and finishes, plus 1 m for rooftop articulation. — [NSW ADG Part 2](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-2-developing-the-controls.pdf)
- London LPG C2.3 requires a 2.5 m ceiling over at least 75% of each dwelling's GIA. C2.4 (best practice) asks for a ground-floor floor-to-floor height of at least 3.5 m so ground floors can be converted to non-residential use. — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)

**Exemplars**
- **Peter Barber, McGrath Road (Stratford, London):**
  - 26 homes in a four-storey perimeter block around a courtyard, reworking Victorian back-to-backs but without their single-aspect rooms.
  - No internal communal circulation: every home has its own street or courtyard door, with large glazing and "generous arched entrances".
  - Every home has a roof terrace.
  - Reported density is about 620 habitable rooms per hectare (secondhand figure).
  — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/03/25/peter-barber-architects-mcgrath-road-housing-london/amp/); [Brick Bulletin](https://www.brick.org.uk/brick-bulletin/mcgrath-road); [Architecture Today](https://architecturetoday.co.uk/court-appeal/)
- **Lacaton & Vassal with Druot and Hutin, Grand Parc Bordeaux (2017):**
  - 530 occupied flats in three 1960s blocks were extended with prefabricated winter gardens and balconies, adding about 3.8 m of depth (about 30 m² per flat) without moving residents out.
  - Mies van der Rohe Award 2019. Lacaton & Vassal won the Pritzker Prize in 2021.
  - The 3.8 m figure comes from a single secondary source (Dezeen).
  — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/03/17/key-projects-anne-lacaton-jean-philippe-vassal-pritzker-prize/amp/); [EU culture site](https://culture.ec.europa.eu/node/3233)
- **Elemental (Alejandro Aravena), Quinta Monroy (Iquique, Chile, about 2004):**
  - The firm built "half of a good house": a row typology with each built segment beside an empty bay of the same size for residents to fill.
  - About 70% of the budget went on keeping families on central land.
  - Sources disagree on the date (2003 or 2004). The "36 m²" quote is unverified.
  — [MoMA, Small Scale Big Change](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2010/smallscalebigchange/projects/quinta_monroy_housing.html); [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/project-gallery/quinta-monroy-housing_o); [Wikipedia (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinta_Monroy)
- **Pinnacle@Duxton (Singapore, ARC Studio with RSP, public HDB housing):**
  - Seven 50-storey towers (about 1,848 flats) linked by continuous sky gardens on the 26th and 50th floors, which act as public space.
  - Awards include the 2010 President's Design Award, the 2010 World Architecture Festival housing prize, the 2010 CTBUH Asia best tall building and the 2011 ULI Global Award.
  — [ULI case study](https://casestudies.uli.org/the-pinnacleduxton); [President's Design Award](https://pda.designsingapore.org/award-recipients/2010/pinnacle-at-duxton/)
- **The Interlace (Singapore, OMA):** cascading sky gardens. Won the inaugural CTBUH Urban Habitat Award in 2014. — [CapitaLand](https://www.capitaland.com/en/about-capitaland/newsroom/news-releases/international/2014/jun/nr-20140627-the-interlace-wins-the-prestigious-inaugural-urban-habitat-award.html)

### Inferences
- **For BuildWise `programmeFor()`:**
  - Cap flats per core per floor at 8; above 8, add a second core or switch to deck access.
  - Treat any corridor longer than about 12 m from the core as needing a window or seating bay, or replace it with an external gallery (deck access). In hot-humid Nigeria, a gallery also gives every flat cross-ventilation.
  - Keep block depth at 12–18 m glass to glass and single-aspect rooms within 2.5 × the ceiling height.
  - For flats: 2 m-deep balconies (2.4 m for 3-bed) off the living room.
  - Ground floor: 3.3–3.5 m floor-to-ceiling where commercial use is possible, otherwise 2.7 m habitable, giving about 3.1 m floor-to-floor for residential storeys.
- **For facades:**
  - Give tall blocks a distinct base (taller ground storey, different material, defined entrance) and a distinct top (setback, roof terrace, parapet change).
  - Group balconies or windows in pairs of floors, and let party walls and slabs show.
  - Mark corners with height or material changes. Use deep reveals and balconies for shadow, not flat wall-to-wall glazing or uniform punched windows.
- **For terraces (row houses):** the McGrath Road and Quinta Monroy lessons suggest own front doors on the street, a private roof terrace or back yard per house, and room for later extension.

### Gaps
- The later GLA update that changes the per-core and dual-aspect standards was not read; the 2023 figures are used here.
- Singapore HDB internal planning rules (corridor lengths, flat sizes) were not found. Only URA balcony rules and the Pinnacle@Duxton facts are covered.
- No primary sources were retrieved for Hong Kong, Japanese, Nordic, Mexican or Brazilian housing standards or exemplars. Examples would be Hong Kong's Buildings Department PNAP APP-151/152, Danish BR18, Japanese *danchi* or Tokyo housing, Mexico's Infonavit or Tatiana Bilbao, and Brazil's Minha Casa Minha Vida or Paulo Mendes da Rocha. They need follow-up research.
- Point block, slab and courtyard typology diagrams were not found in a single citable academic source. The typology names are standard but unsourced here.

## 2. Small offices, mixed-use and retail plazas: ground-floor activation, cores, daylight depth, shading, facades

### Takeaway
Shallow plans and active ground floors are what make these buildings look and work well. Daylight reaches about 6–7.5 m from a side window (roughly 2–2.5 × the window head height), so office wings work best at about 12–15 m deep when lit from both sides. Lively street frontages have many small units and doors, at least 10 and better 15–20 per 100 m. Ground storeys should be tall: 3.3 m clear or more, about 3.5 m or more floor to floor.

### Cited Findings
- A daylighting pattern guide says that at ceiling heights of about 2.7–3.7 m, effective daylight beyond about 7.6 m (25 ft) from a single window wall is "nearly impossible". A 12 m (40 ft) leased span leaves less than half the floor effectively daylit. Depths of about 2.5 × the window head height give balanced brightness. — [Advanced Buildings Pattern Guide, Pattern 3: Section Depth](https://patternguide.advancedbuildings.net/patterns/pattern-3-section-depth)
- A secondary summary of British Council for Offices (BCO) specification tables gives window-to-core depths of about 6–13.5 m, or 6–7.5 m for shallow plans. The source table is garbled, so these figures need checking against the current BCO Guide. — [BCO spec concertina (Gardiner & Theobald)](https://gt-website.files.svdcdn.com/production/publications/1906_BCO-Spec-Concertina.pdf)
- Natural ventilation rules of thumb are single-sided ventilation up to about 2.5 × floor-to-ceiling height in depth, and cross-ventilation up to about 5 ×. These are attributed to CIBSE Guide A and BS 5925 and repeated in the Essex Design Guide. Berkeley CBE is re-testing these limits, so treat them as rules of thumb. — [Essex Design Guide, natural ventilation](https://www.essexdesignguide.co.uk/climate-change/solar-design/natural-ventilation/); [AIVC, Natural ventilation strategies](https://www.aivc.org/sites/default/files/TM_Nat_vent_strategies.pdf); [Berkeley CBE](https://cbe.berkeley.edu/research/evidence-based-natural-ventilation-guidelines/)
- In Gehl Architects' Seattle public-life study, "active" frontage means small units with many doors, 15–20 units per 100 m (328 ft), varied functions and no closed frontage. "Inactive" means 2–5 large units per 100 m. — [Gehl, Seattle Public Spaces & Public Life (Issuu)](https://issuu.com/gehlarchitects/docs/565_seattle_pspl/58)
- The City at Eye Level, which covers Gehl's work, cites a target of at least 10 doorways per 100 m of facade for variety at eye height. — [The City at Eye Level, "Close encounters with buildings"](https://thecityateyelevel.com/stories/close-encounters-with-buildings/)
- The UK Urban Design Compendium defines "Grade A" active frontage as more than 15 premises and more than 25 doors and windows per 100 m. — [Urban Design Compendium](https://people.utm.my/lylai/wp-content/uploads/sites/630/2017/05/Urban-Design-Compendium-1.90.pdf)
- For mixed-use buildings, the ADG recommends 3.3 m ceilings on ground and first floors so uses can change. The building depth steps from a deeper commercial podium to a narrower residential block above; an ADG case study is 18 m residential over 21.6 m commercial. — [NSW ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf); [NSW ADG Part 2](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-2-developing-the-controls.pdf)
- No building separation is needed along main streets or podium levels where buildings share blank party walls. This supports a continuous street wall for shops. — [NSW ADG Part 2](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-2-developing-the-controls.pdf)
- Facade datum lines (awnings, colonnade heights, parapets) should line up with neighbouring buildings. — [NSW ADG Part 4, 4M-1](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)

### Inferences
- **For BuildWise offices:**
  - Use 12–15 m-deep wings with a side core (stair, lift, toilets), so both long faces have usable daylight within about 6–7.5 m. On a wider plot, use an L or a courtyard rather than a deep square.
  - Floor-to-ceiling of about 2.7–3.0 m lets daylight and air reach further than 2.5 m does.
- **For shops and plazas:**
  - Divide the frontage into units about 5–7 m wide, which gives 15–20 per 100 m.
  - Give every unit its own door and glazed front, with a continuous canopy or colonnade at a shared datum height.
  - Put upper-floor entrances between shops rather than at the back.
- **Shading:** in a hot climate like Nigeria, deep overhangs, colonnades and vertical fins on east and west faces are the main articulation tools. No primary source was retrieved for specific shading ratios (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for shading device ratios (overhang depth to window height by latitude) or tropical office design (e.g. Ken Yeang, WOHA, Singapore BCA Green Mark guidance).
- No primary source was found for typical office floor-to-floor heights (commonly cited as 3.6–4.2 m) or the BCO Guide's own figures.
- No source was retrieved for the 5–6 m shop unit width often attributed to Gehl; only the units-per-100 m figures are sourced.

## 3. Hotels, schools and clinics: courtyard and finger plans, natural ventilation, wayfinding, short corridors

### Takeaway
The best low-resource and climate-responsive schools and hospitals use finger or pavilion plans: narrow, two-sided wings about 7–8 m deep, linked by covered outdoor walkways around courtyards and gardens. Raised or double roofs and high ceilings drive natural ventilation, and the landscape is part of the building. Kéré's Gando school and MASS Design Group's Butaro hospital are the reference projects. The UK government's standard school designs use the same finger-block logic with 7.2–7.8 m classroom depths. Hotels are mainly double-loaded slabs on a 3.6–4.5 m room module, so their character comes from the base, the courtyard and the roof rather than the room floors.

### Cited Findings

**Schools**
- The UK DfE baseline school designs are finger-block layouts. Finger blocks are "inherently more flexible in relation to more challenging site shapes and topography", though their wall-to-floor ratio is less efficient than a superblock's. — [DfE baseline designs for schools guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/baseline-designs-for-schools-guidance/baseline-designs-for-schools-guidance)
- DfE baseline classroom dimensions: in the 240-place primary, classrooms are 7.2 m deep and lit from both sides. In the 1,850-place secondary, teaching runs are 7.8 m deep. Voids in the centre of the teaching wings bring daylight into corridors and the backs of classrooms and act as stacks for natural ventilation. In the 420-place primary, ventilation chimneys draw air through the classrooms. — [DfE baseline design 240-place primary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/baseline-design-240-place-primary-school-with-a-26-place-nursery); [DfE baseline design 1,850-place secondary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/baseline-design-1850-place-secondary-school); [DfE baseline design 420-place primary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/baseline-design-420-place-primary-school-with-26-place-nursery)
- The DfE output specification sets daylight targets for each learning space of at least 80% UDI-a and at least 50% sDA. — [DfE Baseline designs daylight strategy](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/388138/Baseline_designs_daylight_strategy_Dec_2014.pdf)
- The DfE Pattern Book (C01, January 2026) describes courtyard gardens as semi-enclosed social, play and dining spaces, with teaching blocks linked by covered walkways. This comes from a search summary; the PDF itself was not read. — [DfE Pattern Book](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6985d6826da2dee8230a9b2f/ER-DFE-XX-XX-T-X-0301-Pattern_Book-A-C01.pdf)
- **Kéré, Gando Primary School (Burkina Faso, 2001, Aga Khan Award 2004):**
  - Clay walls under a double roof: a perforated clay ceiling with a raised, overhanging metal roof above.
  - The gap between the two roofs lets hot air escape, and louvred wall openings bring air in.
  - The wide eaves shade the walls from sun and rain.
  - AKDN reports the interior is up to 6°C cooler than under a tin roof alone (search summary).
  — [Aga Khan Award, Primary School Gando](https://the.akdn/en/architecture/project/primary-school); [MoMA, Small Scale Big Change](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2010/smallscalebigchange/projects/primary_school.html); [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/785955/primary-school-in-gando-kere-architecture)

**Clinics and hospitals**
- **MASS Design Group, Butaro District Hospital (Rwanda, 2011):**
  - 150 beds and about 6,000 m² on a terraced hillside.
  - A campus of linked buildings around an *umuvumu* tree, a traditional gathering place.
  - Covered external corridors run along the building edges, so movement happens in the open air and indoor crowding drops.
  - Wards face outward to the landscape and are designed for daylight, ventilation and views. High ceilings, operable windows, louvres and large slow fans drive natural ventilation, with UV germicidal fixtures.
  - Clinical and non-clinical zones are separated.
  - Local volcanic stone, with shaded outdoor seating where infection risk is lower.
  — [MASS Design Group (Model of Architecture), Butaro District Hospital](https://modelofarchitecture.org/work/design/butaro-district-hospital)
- **Kéré, Léo Surgical Clinic and Health Centre (Burkina Faso, 2014, 1,660 m²):** ten modules arranged like houses in a village along a central street, with surgery, ward and maternity. The doctors' housing added later has five apartments around a shared courtyard. — [Kéré Architecture](https://www.kerearchitecture.com/work/building/surgical-clinic-and-health-centre); [Archilovers](https://archilovers.com/projects/127540/surgical-clinic-in-leo.html)
- At Kéré's Laongo health centre, open-air enclosures help natural ventilation. — [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/5925-center-for-health-care-and-social-promotion)

**Hotels**
- Guest-room floors usually make up 65–85% of a hotel's total area, and double-loaded corridor slabs are a main plan type. This comes from a secondary compilation. — [Design Guide for Hotels (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/doc/269116558/7151503-Design-Guide-for-Hotels)
- Most lower-market hotel rooms are planned on a 3.6 m grid, and higher-end resorts on 4.2–4.5 m. — [Eco-resorts planning and design (Issuu)](https://issuu.com/sulmankhalid/docs/eco-resorts.planning.and.design.for/151)
- A 4-star design-standards deck gives double-loaded corridors of 1.8–2.4 m and a maximum travel distance of 35–40 m (low-quality source). Singapore's URA sets minimum widths for corridors in its hotel retail guidance: 3.0 m for double-loaded main corridors and 2.4 m for others. These apply to retail-related corridors, not guest floors. — [SlideShare 4-star standards](https://fr.slideshare.net/yasminebannoura/4-star-hotels-design-standards); [URA hotel retail units](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/non-residential/hotel/retail-units/)

### Inferences
- **For BuildWise schools and clinics:**
  - Generate finger or pavilion layouts by default: wings about 7–8 m deep, lit and ventilated from both sides, joined by covered external walkways (verandas, which Nigeria's climate favours) around one or more courtyards.
  - Do not generate a single deep block with an internal corridor.
  - Give each wing its own raised or double roof with deep eaves (1 m or more); this is also the visual signature.
  - Clinics: separate the outpatient and waiting courtyard from the wards. Wards face gardens.
- **For hotels:**
  - Accept a double-loaded slab about 15–17 m deep (two 3.6–4.2 m-wide rooms either side of a corridor), but break long slabs into an L or U around a courtyard or pool.
  - Put windows or lounges at corridor ends; the apartment guidance of articulating any corridor longer than 12 m applies by analogy.
  - Give the ground floor a tall, open lobby and veranda.
  - The 15–17 m slab depth is inferred from the room and corridor widths, not sourced directly.

### Gaps
- No Feilden Clegg Bradley school or hospital project page was retrieved.
- No hospital ward depth or nightingale-ward dimension standards were retrieved (UK HBN, WHO guidance).
- No maximum corridor length or wayfinding standard for clinics or schools was found, beyond fire travel distances, which are code-specific.
- Hotel guidance found is mostly secondary or low-quality. No brand standards or primary architectural sources were found.

## 4. Places of worship and event halls: spatial hierarchy, approach and threshold, roof as the main gesture

### Takeaway
Contemporary worship buildings that win awards make the experience a sequence: a raised plinth or forecourt, a threshold (porch, colonnade or courtyard), then a single column-free hall. Light comes from above or through perforated walls, and one strong roof or wall element carries the identity. Tabassum's Bait Ur Rouf mosque does this with no dome or minaret. Event halls follow the same logic: a large clear-span volume under an expressive roof, with a forecourt and covered approach.

### Cited Findings
- **Marina Tabassum, Bait Ur Rouf Mosque (Dhaka, completed 2012, Aga Khan Award 2016):**
  - A single-storey terracotta brick building on a raised plinth that protects against floods and lifts it above a crowded street.
  - No minarets or domes.
  - Light wells, a colonnade and perforated brick walls bring light and air into a column-free prayer hall: load-bearing brick on the outside, a reinforced concrete frame inside.
  - A vertical slit in the brick wall marks the qibla.
  — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2017/03/05/bait-ur-rouf-mosque-dhaka-bangladesh-marina-tabassum-brick-aga-khan-award/amp/); [AKDN](https://the.akdn/en/architecture/project/bait-ur-rouf-mosque); [Domus](https://domusweb.it/en/news/2016/10/04/marina_tabassum_bait_ur_rouf_mosque.html); [World Architects](https://www.world-architects.com/en/architecture-news/works/bait-ur-rouf-mosque)
- A Sicilian parish complex (Santa Barbara) works as both a threshold and a gathering place that connects the parish to its neighbourhood, under a flowing roof. Square openings bring daylight in as both light and symbol. — [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/flowing-roof-fluid-concrete-walls-new-santa-barbara-parish-complex-sicily-francesco-lipari-lillo-giglia-giuseppe-conti/)
- In a Spanish church by AGi Architects (La Ascensión del Señor), the roof "unfolds freely to cover the assembly space, with inclined planes admitting natural light and separating the areas required by liturgy". — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/445306/la-ascension-del-senor-church-agi-architects/)
- ArchDaily's overview of contemporary religious architecture describes projects that rethink traditional worship spaces. Its specific design content was not retrieved. — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/896982/contemporary-religious-architecture-that-rethinks-traditional-spaces-for-worship)
- At Gando, Kéré's raised, overhanging roof, separate from the walls below, is both the climate device and the main architectural gesture. The same logic applies to halls in hot climates. — [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2010/smallscalebigchange/projects/primary_school.html)

### Inferences
- **For BuildWise worship buildings and event halls:**
  - Model the sequence: forecourt or plinth, then a covered threshold (porch or colonnade across the entrance front), then a single clear-span hall, then a focal end (altar, qibla wall, stage).
  - Let the roof carry the identity: a raised, deep-eaved or folded roof, a clerestory, or a lantern over the hall. The walls stay simple and solid or perforated, rather than a flat box with a parapet.
  - Ancillary rooms (offices, toilets, stores, kitchens) go in a lower wing or side band, so the hall reads as the tallest, dominant volume.
  - For a mosque, orient the hall to the qibla.
- **Perforated brick or blockwork screens:** these give light and ventilation and are cheap and locally buildable in Nigeria, matching the Bait Ur Rouf and Gando approaches.

### Gaps
- No design guidance with numbers was found for event halls: clear spans, height-to-span ratios, occupancy per m², and forecourt or parking ratios. These are code-specific.
- No primary sources were retrieved on church or mosque planning dimensions (nave proportions, prayer-row spacing, mosque courtyard ratios).
- Event hall exemplars (e.g. community halls in Africa or South Asia) were not researched.

### Inferences
- **Floor-to-floor heights** derived from the ADG rule: about 3.1 m for residential storeys (2.7 + 0.4) and about 3.7 m for commercial or flexible ground and first floors (3.3 + 0.4). London's best-practice ground floor of at least 3.5 m is consistent with this.
- **Moving the values into the market pack:** these values could go into the market pack's `design` section, as CLAUDE.md requires. For example: `maxUnitsPerCore: 8`, `corridorArticulateAfterM: 12`, `aptDepthM: [12, 18]`, `singleAspectDepthFactor: 2.5`, `crossVentDepthFactor: 5`, `balconyMinDepthM: { '1bed': 2, '2bed': 2, '3bed': 2.4 }`, `classroomDepthM: 7.2`, `shopUnitsPer100m: 15`.
- **Nigeria-specific values:** these should be marked *proposed*, like the rest of `demo-2026.1`.
- **Hot-humid climate:** cross-ventilation matters more in Nigeria than in Sydney or London. The engine should treat dual aspect or cross-ventilation as close to universal for flats, hotels, schools and clinics, using external galleries and verandas rather than internal corridors. This is an inference from the ventilation rules and the Gando and Butaro precedents, not a sourced Nigerian rule.

### Gaps

- Japanese and Nordic housing standards (minimum flat sizes, daylight rules) and Hong Kong's PNAP floor-to-floor and balcony rules were not retrieved.
- Office-specific numbers were not retrieved from primary sources: BCO floor-to-floor, occupancy density, core-to-floor ratio, BCO Guide 2023 window-to-core figures.
- No sourced maximum dead-end or corridor lengths were found for schools, clinics or hotels; these are governed by local fire codes.
