West Africa stays the core. The global section is thinner because fewer tool calls were left for it. ## 1. Principles of tropical modernism (Fry & Drew, Bawa, Correa, Nwoko, Kéré)

### Takeaway
Tropical modernism lets climate generate the form. The recurring moves are:
- Short, solid east and west walls, with the long north and south faces shaded by brise-soleil or deep overhangs.
- Shallow, cross-ventilated rooms behind perforated concrete screens.
- Covered walkways, verandas and eaves deep enough to keep out both sun and driving rain.
- Courtyards and open-to-sky spaces.
- A separate, ventilated roof above the ceiling.

Kéré's double roof and Correa's open-to-sky and tube plans are the clearest contemporary versions of these moves.

### Cited Findings
- **Fry & Drew, orientation and shading:**
  - A review summarises their passive cooling approach as orienting buildings so the short solid walls face east and west, with brise-soleils on the north and south faces to aid cross-ventilation — [Wikipedia: Tropical Modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Modernism) (search summary; the primary text was not fetched).
  - Their buildings had deep overhangs above corridors and walkways, and cross-ventilated rooms with concrete screens — [Frieze: Tropical Modernism and the Promise of the Past](https://www.frieze.com/article/derin-fadina-tropical-modernism-244).
  - In their student housing, blocks were arranged around courtyards so the main blocks could be oriented for the best cross-ventilation — [Academia.edu: Fry & Drew in colonial Nigeria 1946–1959](https://www.academia.edu/31776669/Maxwell_Fry_and_Jane_Drew_Partners_The_Contributions_of_British_Architects_to_Built_Environment_in_Colonial_Nigeria_1946_1959). This source is of uneven quality.
  - The handbook is *Tropical Architecture in the Humid Zone* (1956), later expanded to *Tropical Architecture in the Dry and Humid Zones*. Wikipedia credits it with spreading the principles internationally — [Wikipedia: Tropical Modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Modernism).
- **Koenigsberger et al., *Manual of Tropical Housing and Building* (1974)** (scanned fragments seen through a search summary):
  - Warm-humid climates are treated as a belt near the Equator extending to about 15°N.
  - In warm-humid climates, full enclosure and airtightness are only achievable, and only advisable, with air conditioning.
  - A separate roof and ceiling is the obvious roof for warm-humid climates.
  - Outdoor spaces matter even more in warm-humid regions than in hot-dry ones.
  - Walls and roofs should have low thermal capacity and good insulation.
  - — [Scribd copy of the Manual](https://www.scribd.com/document/470790221/Otto-H-Koenigsberger-Manual-of-Tropical-Housing-and-Building); [Wikipedia: Mahoney tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahoney_tables).
- **Bawa and Correa:**
  - Both hold that climate, not style, should generate form. Correa's open-to-sky spaces and tube houses are described as first cousins of Bawa's courts and verandas — [studioMATRX: Geoffrey Bawa](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architects/geoffrey-bawa) (single blog-type source).
  - Correa pioneered the tube house and the open-to-sky house as climate-responsive plan types — [studioMATRX: Tropical Architecture in India](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/tropical-architecture-india).
  - Bawa used traditional courtyards, overhanging eaves and open-to-sky spaces, and "leaked" the structure into the landscape — [RTF: Geoffrey Bawa's Tropical Modernism](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/know-your-architects/a14954-blending-nature-and-vernacular-geoffrey-bawas-tropical-modernism/).
- **Demas Nwoko (Nigeria):**
  - An artist-architect who combines indigenous materials, cultural symbolism and climate-responsive strategies — [ResearchGate: A Critique of the Design Genre of Demas Nwoko (Fadele)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393453431_A_Critique_of_the_Design_Genre_of_Demas_Nwoko_by_Adewumi_Fadele).
  - His residential work uses sun-control screens, natural cooling and adobe-inspired walls — [Ozi Ikòrò: Demas Nwoko and the Evolution of Nigerian Architecture](https://ozikoro.com/demas-nwoko-and-the-evolution-of-nigerian-architecture/).
- **Diébédo Francis Kéré (Burkina Faso, hot-dry savanna, not humid):**
  - At Gando Primary School, clay walls carry a double roof of adobe and tin that blocks the sun and keeps the inside up to 6 °C cooler than tin alone would — [Aga Khan Award / ArchNet: Primary School, Gando](https://www.akdn.org/architecture/project/primary-school).
  - A corrugated tin roof is raised above the clay ceiling. Hot air rising in the gap draws cooler air up from below (stack effect). Double-skin roofs and façades and greenery are core parts of the design — [Holcim Foundation: Secondary School in Gando](https://www.holcimfoundation.org/projects/secondary-school-with-passive-ventilation-system).
  - Every classroom has openings at both ends for cross-ventilation — [ArchDaily: How Francis Kéré uses materials to respond to climate](https://www.archdaily.com/978851/how-does-francis-kere-use-materials-to-respond-to-local-climate-conditions/).
  - Louvred slats in the walls let air circulate. The design was dictated by the climate and by the aim of building with human power only — [MoMA: Small Scale, Big Change, Primary School](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2010/smallscalebigchange/projects/primary_school.html).
  - Outdoor floors and walls use local laterite stone. Walls are double layers of local concrete blocks and compressed stabilised earth blocks (CSEB) — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/978851/how-does-francis-kere-use-materials-to-respond-to-local-climate-conditions/).
  - Source caveat: some sources mix up the 2001 primary school with the later secondary school, which uses cast clay-concrete rather than bricks — [Holcim Foundation](https://www.holcimfoundation.org/projects/secondary-school-with-passive-ventilation-system).
- **Glenn Murcutt (Australia, mostly temperate):** his own house uses corrugated metal roofs, louvred windows and insect screens, following the idea of "touching the earth lightly" — [studioMATRX / search summary on Murcutt](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architects/geoffrey-bawa) (weak source; the Marika-Alderton House was not covered).

### Inferences
- Rules BuildWise's engine could encode for hot-humid sites:
  - Long axis east-west, so the long faces look north and south.
  - Solid or nearly solid gable walls, or service rooms (stores, WCs, stairs), on the east and west ends.
  - Habitable rooms in single-banked bands, or in plans no more than two rooms deep, with openings on two opposite sides.
  - A veranda or deep eave on at least the main living face.
  - Optionally, a courtyard or open-to-sky space to break up deep plans.
- A ventilated double roof (sheet roof over an insulated ceiling, with a vented gap) is the canonical tropical roof. It maps to BuildWise's pitched-roof-plus-ceiling default, with insulation required by the Nigerian energy code (see section 3).
- Kéré's lessons come from a hot-dry savanna climate (big day-night temperature swings), which is closer to Abuja's or northern Nigeria's than to Lagos's. In Lagos and Port Harcourt (hot-humid, small day-night swing), thermal mass helps less and ventilation plus shading dominate. This is consistent with Koenigsberger's "low thermal capacity" advice for warm-humid climates.

### Gaps
- I could not read Fry & Drew's primary text, so it was not verified whether they explicitly prescribe single-banked plans, specific overhang depths or raised floors. The PDF review on ceon.rs timed out.
- No reliable source was found on Correa's numeric guidance (for example tube-house section ratios) or on Bawa's eave depths.
- Raised floors (as in Malay and Caribbean vernacular) were not sourced in this pass.

## 2. What contemporary Nigerian and West African practices design for homes

### Takeaway
Published, citable material on contemporary West African houses is thin compared with the marketing of high-end Lekki and Ikoyi homes. The best-documented voices are Tosin Oshinowo (passive cooling first, local materials, Yoruba public-to-private spatial layering) and the heritage of Nwoko and Fry & Drew. Luxury Lagos houses favour interlocking cantilevered volumes, and several commentators observe that breeze-block screens and courtyards have been lost to maximise sellable floor area.

### Cited Findings
- **Tosin Oshinowo / Oshinowo Studio (formerly CM Design Atelier), Lagos:**
  - Her West African practice puts passive cooling before mechanical systems, uses local materials and craft wherever possible, and designs buildings that can adapt to changing use — [ArchDaily: Tosin Oshinowo, Redefining African Architecture](https://www.archdaily.com/1024037/tosin-oshinowo-redefining-african-architecture-through-context-and-collaboration).
  - Her Lantern House reinterprets Yoruba spatial organisation in a contemporary form, around the layered public and private spaces of Nigerian family life — [ArchDaily (same)](https://www.archdaily.com/1024037/tosin-oshinowo-redefining-african-architecture-through-context-and-collaboration); see also [Oshinowo Studio: About](https://www.oshinowostudio.com/about-oshinowo-studio/) and [Wikipedia: Tosin Oshinowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosin_Oshinowo).
- **High-end Lekki house (elArc Designs):** a two-storey house of about 1,230 m², composed of interlocking volumes with clean lines and carefully proportioned cantilevers so that it does not read as one monolithic block — [elArc Designs: Lekki home project](https://elarcdesigns.com/projects-item/burna-boy-lekki-home/) (firm's own marketing page).
- **Ikoyi family residence:** arranged around a tropical courtyard, with covered terraces and deep eaves for both rainy and dry seasons — [SATU: Ikoyi Family Residence](https://www.interiordesignabudhabiuae.com/ikoyi-family-residence.html) (firm marketing page).
- **Loss of mid-century elements:** mid-century perforated concrete blocks gave privacy without isolation. Modern builds have largely replaced them with solid walls, and courtyards have been cut from many designs to maximise sellable floor area — [Realtor Kingz: Lost arts of Nigerian architecture](https://realtorkingz.com/lost-arts-of-nigerian-architecture/) (blog; opinion).
- **Ikoyi** is commonly split into Ikoyi I (older, more traditional buildings) and Ikoyi II (newer high-rise and luxury developments) — [Wikipedia: Ikoyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikoyi).

### Inferences
- For the Nigerian upper market, a credible "contemporary" engine output is:
  - A composition of offset, interlocking rectangular volumes with flat parapet or concealed roofs and cantilevered upper floors that shade the floor below.
  - A courtyard or covered terrace as the social heart.
  - Breeze-block or louvre screens on stairs, service yards and west faces.

  This merges the market's taste (volumes and cantilevers) with the climate logic in section 1.
- Oshinowo's "layered public-to-private" maps directly onto BuildWise's room zoning: a visitors' or formal sitting room and guest WC near the entrance, a family lounge deeper in, bedrooms most private, and a boys' quarters (outbuilding) as a separate block. This matches the engine's existing `outbuilding` concept.

### Gaps
- Material on these practices was not found or not fetched in this pass: studioMANIFOLD, NLÉ / Kunle Adeyemi (mostly non-residential: Makoko Floating School), Mariam Kamara / atelier masomi (Niger, mostly civic), Studio Contra, SAOTA's West African houses, James Cubitt, and Patrick Waheed.
- There are no peer-reviewed studies of the typical plan, massing or material palette of Lekki, Banana Island, Maitama or Asokoro houses. Those claims rest only on firm marketing pages.
- Accra and Port Harcourt-specific residential case studies were not found.

## 3. Quantitative climate guidance (room depth, WWR, overhangs, ceilings, courtyards, orientation)

### Takeaway
The hard numbers available:
- Nigeria's 2017 Building Energy Efficiency Code (BEEC) caps window-to-wall ratio at 20% per orientation unless the glazing is adequately shaded, and requires roof insulation of at least R 1.25 m²K/W.
- The widely used ventilation rules of thumb limit room depth to 5× floor-to-ceiling height for cross-ventilation and 2.5× for single-sided ventilation.
- The Australian eave rule (eave width ≈ 45% of sill-to-eave height) was written for winter-sun climates. In the tropics it should be treated as a minimum.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigeria BEEC (Federal Ministry of Power, Works and Housing, first edition, August 2017).** Read directly from the PDF text — [National Building Energy Efficiency Code (PDF)](https://engineersforum.com.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Building-Energy-Efficiency-Code.pdf):
  - §3.1.1: minimum requirements apply to all climatic zones in Nigeria.
  - §3.1.2: interventions are based on the bioclimatic principles of the National Building Energy Efficiency Guideline (BEEG). They include reducing the overall window-to-wall ratio or adding shading, and minimum roof insulation.
  - §3.2.1.1: "Window to Wall Ratio for any orientation shall not exceed 20%." Where that cannot be achieved, all glazing on the relevant façades must be adequately shaded. Compliance is shown with the BEEC Calculator.
  - §3.2.2.1: lighting power density must not exceed 6 W/m² for residential and 8 W/m² for offices.
  - §3.2.3.1: all roof constructions must include insulation with R ≥ 1.25 m²K/W.
  - §3.1.4: there are two compliance routes. The prescriptive route is a checklist. The performance route requires simulated energy use no greater than that of the same building with all the prescriptive measures.
  - The star rating table repeats the 20% WWR (or shading per the BEEC Calculator) and R 1.25 roof insulation for 1 to 4 stars.
- **BEEC shading depth:** one paper says the code recommends horizontal shading 500 mm deep, citing FMPWH (2017) — [search summary of a 2019 Lokoja simulation study, Journal of Engineering and Architecture 7(1)](https://jea.thebrpi.org/journals/jea/Vol_7_No_1_June_2019/6.pdf). I did not find a 500 mm figure in the code text itself, so it may come from the BEEG guideline or the calculator. Treat it as unverified.
- **Proposed revision:** a ResearchGate paper proposes raising the minimum WWR to at least 25% and setting a 1,350 mm minimum window width for Nigerian hot-humid ventilation — [ResearchGate: Reevaluating Window Design Standards in Nigerian Architecture (≥1350 mm)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395424729_Reevaluating_Window_Design_Standards_in_Nigerian_Architecture_Optimizing_Width_1350mm_and_Ventilation_Efficiency_for_Hot-Humid_Climates). This is a proposal, not code, and it pulls against the BEEC's 20% maximum. The tension is that more opening helps ventilation, while more glazing adds solar gain unless shaded.
- **Code development and cooling:** [MDPI: Transition towards Energy Efficiency, Developing the Nigerian BEEC](https://www.mdpi.com/320090); [SEforALL: Sustainable cooling for Nigeria's buildings](https://www.seforall.org/data-stories/sustainable-cooling-for-nigerias-buildings-and-cities). A search summary says a demonstration project found window film plus shading could cut cooling demand by about 20%. I did not verify this on the page.
- **Ventilation depth rules:**
  - Cross-ventilated room depth should not exceed 5× room height, and single-sided 2.5× — [Essex Design Guide: Natural ventilation](https://www.essexdesignguide.co.uk/climate-change/solar-orientation/natural-ventilation/).
  - Cross ventilation suits buildings up to about 12 to 15 m deep (5× floor-to-ceiling height), or 2.5× with openings on one side only — [Designing Buildings Wiki: Cross ventilation](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Cross_ventilation).
  - The rules are often attributed to CIBSE, but I found no CIBSE document confirming them.
- **Eave rule (Australia, YourHome):**
  - Eave width should be about 45% of the height from the bottom window sill to the underside of the eave.
  - Tabulated overhangs: 450 mm for 900–1,200 mm height, 600 mm for 1,200–1,350 mm, 900 mm for 1,350–2,100 mm, and 1,200 mm for 2,100–2,700 mm.
  - More shading suits warmer tropical climates.
  - West faces need vertical shading (screens, planting) because low afternoon sun gets under any eave.
  - — [YourHome: Shading](https://www.yourhome.gov.au/passive-design/shading) (seen through a search summary; the page fetch timed out; the table was quoted via a [HomeOne forum post](https://forum.homeone.com.au/viewtopic.php?f=31&t=100266)).
  - Climate caveat: the rule is designed for north-facing glazing in southern-hemisphere temperate climates, where winter sun is wanted. At 6–9°N the sun passes nearly overhead, north of zenith for part of the year (roughly May to August) and south for the rest. Both the north and south faces therefore need overhangs.
- **Abuja passive design study:** [Sustainability 17(18):8435, "Improving the Thermal Environment of Abuja's Affordable Housing Through Passive Design Solutions"](https://doi.org/10.3390/su17188435). It exists, but the fetch returned 403, so its numeric results are not recorded here.
- **Courtyard proportions (hot-dry):** I found no reliable source for an optimal courtyard height-to-width ratio. The only pages giving numbers (H/W ≈ 1.5; 1.0–1.6; 0.6–0.9) were uncited blogs that contradict each other — [agricreatorbook courtyard guide](https://www.agricreatorbook.com/2026/05/courtyard-house-passive-cooling%20%20%20.html); [nuviraspace](https://nuviraspace.com/courtyard-house-hot-dry-climate/). They are not usable as rules.

### Inferences
- Encodable rules for BuildWise (Nigeria pack):
  - (a) WWR ≤ 20% per façade by default. When a style asks for "large" or "full-height" glazing (`Room.glazing`), the engine should add shading on that façade (canopy, balcony, deep eave or screen) and say so in the notes. That is the BEEC route.
  - (b) Roof insulation R ≥ 1.25 m²K/W as a bill-of-materials line for every roof.
  - (c) Cross-ventilated plan depth ≤ 5 × ceiling height: with a 3.0 m ceiling, a room or flat cross-section ≤ 15 m. A room ventilated from one side only should be ≤ 2.5 × ceiling height (7.5 m at a 3 m ceiling).
  - (d) Eaves on north and south faces of at least about 0.45 × sill-to-eave height (≈ 0.9–1.2 m for standard windows). For flat parapet roofs, use canopies or balconies of a similar depth.
  - (e) West glazing minimised, or screened vertically.
- The 500 mm BEEC shading figure, if real, is much shallower than the Australian table. For a generator, the deeper value (≥ 0.9 m) is the safer default in the humid south, where it also protects openings from driving rain.

### Gaps
- No sourced recommended ceiling height for Nigerian houses was found in this pass. Nigerian building regulations probably set minimum habitable-room heights, but they were not located.
- No Nigerian-specific ideal WWR for ventilation, prevailing wind directions for Lagos or Abuja, or BEEG content (climate zones, orientation tables) was retrieved.
- The BEEC Calculator's shading method (projection-factor thresholds) was not found.

## 4. Materials and colours in contemporary West African homes

### Takeaway
Documented low-carbon, climate-responsive palettes use laterite, compressed earth blocks, clay and corrugated metal over ventilated ceilings (Kéré), and adobe-inspired walls with sun screens (Nwoko). The mainstream Nigerian market favours sandcrete-block walls with render, solid parapets and few screens. No rigorous survey of colours and finishes was found.

### Cited Findings
- Kéré uses local laterite stone, double-layer walls of concrete blocks and compressed stabilised earth blocks, and clay pots set into a concrete roof for light and air in the Gando library — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/978851/how-does-francis-kere-use-materials-to-respond-to-local-climate-conditions/).
- His roof is corrugated tin raised over a clay ceiling — [Holcim Foundation](https://www.holcimfoundation.org/projects/secondary-school-with-passive-ventilation-system); [Aga Khan Award](https://www.akdn.org/architecture/project/primary-school).
- Nwoko uses adobe-inspired walls and sun-control screens — [Ozi Ikòrò](https://ozikoro.com/demas-nwoko-and-the-evolution-of-nigerian-architecture/).
- Oshinowo prefers local materials and craftsmanship wherever possible — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1024037/tosin-oshinowo-redefining-african-architecture-through-context-and-collaboration).
- Mid-century Nigerian perforated concrete blocks gave privacy with airflow; modern houses mostly use solid walls — [Realtor Kingz](https://realtorkingz.com/lost-arts-of-nigerian-architecture/) (opinion blog).
- Koenigsberger favours low thermal capacity and well-insulated envelopes for warm-humid seasons — [Scribd copy of the Manual](https://www.scribd.com/document/470790221/Otto-H-Koenigsberger-Manual-of-Tropical-Housing-and-Building) (fragment).
- The Brazilian parallel: brise-soleil came as vertical or horizontal panels, fixed or movable, in reinforced concrete, asbestos cement, metal or timber. Smaller cellular elements (cobogó, claustra) were made in precast concrete or ceramic. Ceramic wall facings and tropical planting were part of the designs — [Rytel, Brazilian modernism and climate (PAN)](https://oldkaiu.pan.pl/images/4.2013pdf/G.Rytel_eng.pdf); [Pedro Guedes, Behind the veils of modern tropical architecture](https://edisciplinas.usp.br/pluginfile.php/5674870/mod_resource/content/0/Pedro%20Guedes-Behind%20the%20veils%20of%20modern%20tropical%20architecture.pdf).

### Inferences
- A sensible "contemporary West African" `MATERIALS` set for the engine:
  - Light render on blockwork for main walls.
  - Laterite, or laterite-toned render or stone, for plinths and feature walls.
  - Breeze blocks (claustra) for screens.
  - Timber or aluminium louvres.
  - Terrazzo floors.
  - Long-span metal sheet (pitched) or concealed metal or concrete roofs behind parapets.
- These are consistent with the sources above, but no single source lists this palette, so present it as a proposal.
- Light-coloured roofs and walls cut solar gain. That is a standard principle, but I did not find a Nigerian source in this pass, so it is a gap for citation.

### Gaps
- No market survey or academic source was found on the prevalence of parapet versus pitched roofs in Lagos and Abuja, on terrazzo or render colours, or on roof colour and reflectance requirements in Nigeria (BEEC sets none in the prescriptive route).

## 5. Global climate-responsive house design (scope extension)

### Takeaway
Across climates the levers are the same (orientation, shading, ventilation, glazing ratio, thermal mass, insulation, roof form), but their settings flip:
- **Hot-humid:** shade everything, open the plan to the breeze, use light materials and lifted roofs.
- **Hot-dry:** thick massive walls, small openings, shaded courtyards, wind catchers, night cooling.
- **Mediterranean:** a hybrid, with seasonal shading, mass and night ventilation.
- **Temperate and cold:** compact, airtight, well insulated, with glazing concentrated on the sun side (Passivhaus).

### Cited Findings
**Hot-humid tropics (Sri Lanka, India, Brazil, Southeast Asia, Caribbean, coastal West Africa)**
- Sri Lanka and India: see section 1 (Bawa: courtyards, deep eaves, open-to-sky spaces; Correa: tube house, open-to-sky house) — [studioMATRX](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/tropical-architecture-india); [RTF](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/know-your-architects/a14954-blending-nature-and-vernacular-geoffrey-bawas-tropical-modernism/).
- Brazil:
  - Modernists enriched the International Style with sun shades, ceramic wall facings and tropical planting as integral parts of the design — [Rytel (PAN)](https://oldkaiu.pan.pl/images/4.2013pdf/G.Rytel_eng.pdf).
  - The Ministry of Education and Health in Rio was one of the first glass-enclosed buildings with an egg-crate brise-soleil — [Rytel (PAN)](https://oldkaiu.pan.pl/images/4.2013pdf/G.Rytel_eng.pdf).
  - Reinforced concrete and the mild climate let Brazilian architects create transparent continuity between inside and outside — [Britannica: Latin American architecture, modern](https://www.Britannica.Com/art/Latin-American-architecture/The-birth-of-modern-architecture-c-1929-65).
- Australia: Murcutt uses corrugated metal roofs, louvred windows and insect screens — [studioMATRX/search summary](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architects/geoffrey-bawa) (weak).
- Shading in the tropics: more shading suits warmer tropical climates (YourHome) — [YourHome: Shading](https://www.yourhome.gov.au/passive-design/shading).
- Koenigsberger:
  - Warm-humid climates need a separate roof and ceiling and outdoor living spaces.
  - Airtightness only makes sense with air conditioning.
  - Light, insulating walls and roofs.
  - The warm-humid belt extends to about 15°N.
  - — [Manual (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/470790221/Otto-H-Koenigsberger-Manual-of-Tropical-Housing-and-Building).

**Hot-dry (Middle East, North Africa, Sahel, Mexico, inland Australia)**
- Hassan Fathy's integrated system: a courtyard, a mashrabiya, and a wind catcher (malqaf) whose upper vent faces the prevailing wind. Cooled courtyard air moves through the house — [ResearchGate: Wind catcher in traditional Cairene courtyard houses](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362712168_Evaluation_of_the_Wind_Catcher_in_the_Traditional_Cairene_Courtyard_Houses'_Integrated_Passive_System_for_Natural_Ventilation_and_Cooling); [Performance of passive design in hot-dry Cairo](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320072339_Performance_of_Passive_Design_Architecture_Application_in_Hot_and_Dry_Climate_Case_study_Cairo_Egypt).
- The mashrabiya is a carved timber lattice over windows or balconies that combines privacy, shading and evaporative cooling. Shaded open spaces are very desirable in hot-dry zones — [arXiv: Systematic review of passive cooling in arid urban environments](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09365); [Traditional ways of dealing with climate in Egypt (CIB)](https://www.irbnet.de/daten/iconda/CIB22610.pdf).
- Exemplars: Hassan Fathy (Egypt) — [Atlantis Press: Hassan Fathy's rural development practices](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125997936.pdf). Kéré (Burkina Faso; section 1).

**Mediterranean**
- The Passive House Institute has a Mediterranean section noting that south orientation matters more there than in Germany — [passivehouse.com: Passive Houses for different climate zones](https://passivehouse.com/02_informations/05_ph-mediterranean/05_ph-mediterranean.htm) (seen in a search result; the page fetch returned 404).
- Vernacular climate strategies in southern Portugal and northern Egypt are compared in a Lund University study — [Lund University PDF](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/files/6264593/4697961.pdf). Passive cooling in eastern Mediterranean (Cyprus) vernacular is studied in [Ozarisoy, Energy & Buildings (UEL repository)](https://repository.uel.ac.uk/download/43ef384fa3d462a520271e4b6ce34d88f51c1e32931575c56d07c02edd3672db/15264421/OZARISOY_Energy%26Buildings-Accepted%20Manuscript.pdf). Contents were not read.
- Bioclimatic and vernacular passive heating and cooling technologies are reviewed in [Energies 17(5):1006](https://doi.org/10.3390/en17051006) (not read).

**Temperate and cold (UK and Europe, Nordic countries, Canada, Japan)**
- Passivhaus:
  - Heating demand ≤ 15 kWh/m²·a, or peak heating load ≤ 10 W/m² — [Passivhaus (Encyclopedia, MDPI)](https://doi.org/10.3390/encyclopedia1010005); [Elemental Solutions: heat load as a design target](https://www.elementalsolutions.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Passivhaus-design-for-heat-load.pdf).
  - Airtightness ≤ 0.6 air changes per hour at 50 Pa — [learnarchitecture.net Passivhaus guide](https://learnarchitecture.net/sustainability/green-building/36120-passivhaus-design-guide.html) (secondary).
- Compact form with a low surface-to-volume ratio reduces thermal bridging and heat loss — [Passivhaus Trust: How to Build a Passivhaus, ch. 7](https://passivhaustrust.org.uk/UserFiles/File/Technical%20Papers/How%20to%20Build%202023/HowToBuild-Ch7.pdf).
- Glazing:
  - Passivhaus Trust (UK) suggests at most about 20% of a south-facing room's internal wall area as glazing, with total glazing about 15–20% of the treated floor area. It prefers low U-values over high solar gain (g-value) — [Passivhaus Trust ch. 7](https://passivhaustrust.org.uk/UserFiles/File/Technical%20Papers/How%20to%20Build%202023/HowToBuild-Ch7.pdf).
  - Levitt Bernstein's guide gives a different split: north 10–15%, south 20–30% — [Levitt Bernstein Passivhaus EASI guide](https://www.levittbernstein.co.uk/site/assets/files/3553/passivhaus-easi-guide_screen_portrait.pdf). The two sources conflict, partly because they measure against different bases (wall area against façade).
  - A review found that more south- and west-facing glazing raised cooling loads by up to 35%, a caution for warm and mixed climates — [Passivhaus (Encyclopedia, MDPI)](https://doi.org/10.3390/encyclopedia1010005) (as reported in a search summary).
- Japan: the engawa (edge veranda) is a traditional transitional zone. Traditional Japanese houses adapt to humidity rather than resisting it — [Houzz: traditional design and the elements](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/how-traditional-design-can-protect-modern-homes-from-the-elements-stsetivw-vs~159711827); [Arch2O: vernacular architecture that breathes](https://www.arch2o.com/living-buildings-how-vernacular-architecture-creates-spaces-that-breathe-with-nature/) (popular sources).
- Nordic: timber-framed Scandinavian buildings place windows to work with northern light patterns — [Arch2O](https://www.arch2o.com/living-buildings-how-vernacular-architecture-creates-spaces-that-breathe-with-nature/) (weak, generic).

### Inferences
- The engine could hold a per-climate parameter set in the market pack's `design` section:
  - **Hot-humid:** WWR ≤ 20% unshaded (BEEC); depth ≤ 5H cross-ventilated; eaves ≥ 0.45 × sill-to-eave on north and south; screens on the west; ventilated roof plus R 1.25 insulation; verandas and courtyards favoured.
  - **Hot-dry:** inward-facing courtyard plans, small external openings, heavy walls, and shaded or screened openings.
  - **Mediterranean:** south-facing living areas, seasonal shading on the south, shutters, night ventilation.
  - **Temperate and cold:** a compact form factor, south glazing about 20–30% of the façade, about 10–15% on the north, airtightness, and little shading on the sun side except against summer overheating.
- Orientation rules must follow the hemisphere and latitude. The "sun side" is south in the northern temperate zone, north in the southern temperate zone, and almost overhead near the Equator, where east and west are the faces to protect.
