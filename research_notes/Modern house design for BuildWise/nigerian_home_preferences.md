# Household expectations and house-plan organisation: Nigeria/West Africa as the worked case, compared globally

Scope note: the brief began Nigeria-only and was broadened mid-task to global (US, UK/Europe, Gulf, India, China/East Asia, Latin America, Australia, Nigeria/West Africa). Nigeria remains the deepest section. Many sources are real-estate blogs, developer brochures or listing sites; peer-reviewed or official sources are flagged where available. Where a claim came only from a search-result snippet (the page itself could not be fetched), it is marked "(snippet)".

## 1. Typical programme of modern Nigerian bungalows and duplexes (3–6 bedrooms)

### Takeaway
The recognisable Nigerian duplex programme is: ground floor = main/visitors' lounge, dining, kitchen (often with store), visitors' toilet, and a ground-floor guest bedroom (usually en-suite); first floor = family lounge, master suite and other en-suite bedrooms, with terraces/balconies; plus a boys' quarters (BQ) as a separate small building at the back of the compound. Survey evidence from Abuja shows strong demand for dining space, en-suite bedrooms, stores, ante rooms and visitors' toilets, and weaker demand for laundry rooms, studies and prayer rooms.

### Cited Findings
- Nigeria Property Centre's glossary: "The ground floor typically has the sitting and dining rooms, kitchen, a guest toilet and sometimes a guest bedroom"; "the upper floor has the main bedrooms"; "Many duplexes come with a boys' quarters"; "The BQ, if there is one, is usually a separate small building at the back of the compound." Duplex types: detached, semi-detached, terraced. — [Nigeria Property Centre, "Duplex" glossary](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/glossary/duplex)
- A contemporary 4-bedroom duplex in Lagos (architect's portfolio): ground floor = main lounge, visitor's toilet, kitchen, dining, guest bedroom en-suite, and BQ en-suite; first floor = family lounge, master bedroom en-suite and two other rooms, each with a terrace; a void looks down into the ground-floor main lounge; top level = open-air roof terrace. — [Archinect, Olateju Joshua Abodunrin, "4 bedroom duplex"](https://archinect.com/Olateju-Joshua-Abodunrin/project/4-bedroom-duplex)
- A typical 5-bedroom Nigerian duplex layout puts 1–2 guest bedrooms, living/dining, kitchen, utility room and guest toilet on the ground floor, and 3–4 bedrooms including the master suite, a family lounge and possibly a study on the first floor (snippet). — [Homestyler, "Transforming five-bedroom duplex designs in Nigeria"](https://www.homestyler.com/article/transforming-fivebedroom-duplex-designs-in-nigeria)
- A commercial Nigerian designer says BQs fit on larger plots without compromising the main house; a 5-bedroom duplex package lists a BQ/staff room plus service areas and parking for 4–6 cars (snippet; SEO-style source). — [Dutum Group, "Modern duplex house designs in Nigeria"](https://dutumgroup.com/modern-duplex-house-designs-in-nigeria/)
- Peer-reviewed survey of 2,133 federal civil servants in Abuja (Akewusola & Firdaus, University of Abuja, 2026) on nine optional interior spaces (dining, laundry, store, en-suite bedroom, toilet separate from bedroom, study, prayer room, ante room, visitors' toilet): strong preferences for **dining spaces, en-suite bedrooms, stores, ante rooms and visitors' toilets** (linked to functionality, privacy and social interaction); **laundry spaces, study rooms, prayer rooms and toilets separated from bedrooms were less preferred**; income significantly shapes preferences for most spaces (chi-square). Recommends flexible, user-centred designs and "tradable" interior spaces. Per-space percentages were not visible on the abstract page. — [Akewusola & Firdaus, UJEES vol. 8 no. 1 (2026)](https://journals.uniosun.edu.ng/ujees/article/view/492)
- Contemporary Nigerian houses (SW Nigeria, Lagos, Abuja) are typically two-storey with in-house bathrooms and kitchens, carport or garage, hard landscaping, air-conditioning, sliding or casement windows, sandcrete-block walls; designated kitchens replaced cooking in corridors. — [Osasona, "Sustainable Homes in Nigeria", Buildings & Cities, 5 Dec 2023](https://buildingsandcities.org/insights/commentaries/sustainable-homes-nigeria.html)
- Boreholes are a necessity where public water supply is unreliable; drilling cost roughly ₦500,000–₦1.5 million (snippet). — [The Guardian Nigeria, "What Are Nigerian Homes Like?"](https://guardian.ng/?p=2824594)

### Inferences
- A default Nigerian duplex programme for the engine: GF {entrance/ante lobby, visitors' lounge, dining, kitchen + store, visitors' WC, guest bedroom en-suite}, FF {family lounge, master suite (en-suite + walk-in closet), 2–3 en-suite bedrooms, balcony/terrace}, external {BQ at rear of compound, parking for several cars}. "Every bedroom en-suite" appears to be the market norm in the sources (each example bedroom is en-suite), which the Abuja survey corroborates.
- Because laundry, study and prayer rooms rank lower in Abuja, they suit "optional extras" rather than defaults; store and ante room rank high and should be defaults.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found specifically on outdoor/"dirty" kitchens, generator houses, gatehouses/security posts, inverter/solar rooms, or walk-in closets in Nigerian plans (the dirty-kitchen and inverter-room conventions are widely referred to informally, but I found no citable page).
- No bungalow-specific programme source; percentages from the Abuja survey were not retrievable.

## 2. How Nigerian plans are arranged (entrance, visitors vs family, bedroom access, kitchen, privacy)

### Takeaway
The sources consistently separate a formal visitors' zone near the entrance (main lounge + visitors' WC, often a guest room) from a private family zone deeper in or upstairs (family lounge, bedrooms). Visual and family privacy from visitors is a documented concern in Nigerian housing research, and the lack of a dedicated guest room and poor placement of spaces and openings are cited causes.

### Cited Findings
- Formal living/dining is placed near the entrance for entertaining and can be closed off; the family living space goes deeper in the home for daily use (snippet). — [Homestyler, five-bedroom duplex designs in Nigeria](https://www.homestyler.com/article/transforming-fivebedroom-duplex-designs-in-nigeria)
- Lagos duplex example: visitors' toilet and main lounge on the ground floor; the family lounge sits on the first floor with the master and other bedrooms (i.e., bedrooms are reached via the upstairs family lounge/landing). — [Archinect, 4 bedroom duplex](https://archinect.com/Olateju-Joshua-Abodunrin/project/4-bedroom-duplex)
- Ibadan public housing study (Amao & Ilesanmi, Urban, Planning and Transport Research, Dec 2022): 50.38% of residents perceived privacy as low or very low, with visual privacy rated lowest; positioning of spaces, orientation of openings and absence of a dedicated guest room contributed to low visual and family privacy (abstract snippet; full page returned 403). — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/d60f184b4c854a61871cd197fbec0e5f)
- Ante rooms (a lobby/anteroom before the living spaces) are among the most strongly preferred optional spaces in Abuja. — [Akewusola & Firdaus 2026](https://journals.uniosun.edu.ng/ujees/article/view/492)
- Traditional Nigerian (Yoruba) houses used impluvium courtyards for aeration, daylight, rainwater and family interaction, front porches for trade and social life, and wide corridors used for activities such as hairdressing and cooking; modern design has lost these. (Traditional pattern.) — [Osasona 2023](https://buildingsandcities.org/insights/commentaries/sustainable-homes-nigeria.html)

### Inferences
- Plan rule set for Nigeria: entrance → foyer/ante lobby → visitors' lounge (with visitors' WC directly off the lobby so guests never enter family areas); family lounge/dining behind or upstairs; bedrooms reached from family lounge/landing, never through the visitors' lounge; kitchen at the rear with its own back door to the service yard and BQ.
- The Ibadan finding suggests placing guest-room windows and visitors' sightlines so they do not look into bedrooms/family lounge.

### Gaps
- No academic source found that maps circulation (e.g. lobby vs landing vs lounge access to bedrooms) quantitatively; kitchen back-entrance orientation is inferred, not cited for Nigeria (it is cited for the Gulf; see section 6).

## 3. Plot sizes, setbacks and coverage (Lagos, Abuja) and how they shape footprints

### Takeaway
A Lagos "full plot" is nominally 60×120 ft but is quoted variously as 648 m² (18×36 m), ~669 m² (exact conversion), or 500–600 m² in many estates; outside Lagos 50×100 ft (~464 m²) is common; Abuja plots vary (~600–1,000 m²). Lagos regulations cap building footprint at 50% (low density), 60% (medium) and 70% (high), with 20%/10%/10% greenspace. I could not find citable residential setback distances for Lagos or Abuja.

### Cited Findings
- Lagos regulation summary (Fadera & Olaniyi, University of Lagos, Acta Horticulturae et Regiotecturae 25(1), 2022, Table 1, citing the Lagos State building regulation and linking the revised LASPPPA 2019 regulation): low density plot 1,000 m², max footprint coverage 50%, 30 dwelling units/ha, 20% greenspace; medium density 864 m², 60%, 90 du/ha, 10%; high density 648 m², 70%, 270 du/ha, 10%. Private open space : footprint = 50:50 (low), 40:60 (medium), 30:70 (high). Their survey of three medium-density Lagos estates found actual coverage ~70% footprint / 30% open space versus the 60/40 rule. — [Fadera & Olaniyi 2022 (PDF)](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/ahr-2022-0008.pdf)
- The governing Lagos regime is the Lagos State Physical Planning Permit and Building Control Regulations 2019 (LASPPPA issues permits, LASBCA enforces), which cover setbacks, building coverage, parking and height limits (snippet). — [Mondaq, pre-construction approvals in Lagos](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/construction-planning/1052184/pre-construction-approvals-and-building-permit-in-lagos-state)
- Lagos full plot commonly 60×120 ft; sellers often quote 18×36 m = 648 m²; exact conversion ~669 m²; many Lagos estates use 500 or 600 m²; some newer estates 900 m² (30×30 m); outside Lagos 50×100 ft (~450–465 m²) is common; Abuja FCTA plots vary (600–1,000 m² in residential districts cited). — [Nigeria Property Centre, plot of land](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/glossary/plot-of-land); [Legit.ng, plots per acre](https://www.legit.ng/1116075-how-plots-acre-nigeria.html); [PropertyPro, land sizes](https://propertypro.ng/blog/?p=4672)
- FCT Development Control enforces approvals and has declined plans near high-tension lines and stream channels; illegal extensions are listed as violations (no setback numbers found). — [AGIS FCTA, land use violations](https://agis.fcta.gov.ng/?p=73); [Nairametrics, Guzape demolitions 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/05/06/fcta-orders-demolition-of-24-two-storey-buildings-on-waterway-in-guzape-district/)

### Inferences
- On a 648 m² medium-density Lagos plot, 60% coverage allows ~389 m² footprint; on a 50×100 ft (~464 m²) plot outside Lagos, far less once a BQ, parking for several cars, and the gate/fence zone are carved out. Footprint is in practice constrained by setbacks + car parking + BQ rather than coverage alone, which favours two-storey duplexes over sprawling bungalows on standard plots.
- Plot dimensions to offer in the engine: 15×30 m (50×100 ft), 18×36 m (Lagos "plot"), 30×30 m (estate), and larger Abuja plots.

### Gaps
- No primary-source residential setback distances (front/side/rear) for Lagos 2019 regulations or FCDA guidelines were found; the official LASPPPA PDF linked by Fadera & Olaniyi was not fetched. Do not hard-code setbacks from these notes.

## 4. Aesthetic styles and criticisms of common Nigerian house design

### Takeaway
Critics describe a homogenised market of "white boxes" and fortress-like blockwork, increasingly small decorative windows, and loss of vernacular climate features (deep eaves, louvres, courtyards, verandas), which raises overheating and AC dependence. I found no citable source defining "Lekki style" or quantifying parapet-roof popularity.

### Cited Findings
- Architects warn about the growing preference for small/compact windows used as façade elements for aesthetics rather than function; natural light is "often the first casualty", and wider windows have more ventilation potential; this pushes residents to mechanical cooling (snippet). — [The Guardian Nigeria, "Climate concerns trail growing preference for compact window designs"](https://guardian.ng/property/climate-concerns-trail-growing-preference-for-compact-window-designs/)
- Critics argue ~95% of new residential developments "appear like white boxes, bereft of unique aesthetic design", with developers prioritising returns over architectural quality (snippet). — [The Guardian Nigeria (property)](https://guardian.ng/?p=2949777)
- A columnist describes Nigerian houses as "fortresses, strongholds and monoliths of blockwork and high-pitched roofs" and occupants as indifferent to poor, user-unfriendly design (snippet). — [Vanguard, Ugoji Egbujo](https://www.vanguardngr.com/?p=2651258)
- Modern Nigerian forms: extra-steep roofs with cornice eaves and high ceilings (British roofing tradition), casement replacing louvre windows, loss of deep eaves → higher overheating risk, AC reliance, reduced ventilation; steep roofs speed runoff; sandcrete lacks earth walls' thermal benefit. Vernacular features worth reviving: deep eaves, louvres, impluvium courtyards, verandas/porches, raised floors (colonial/Brazilian influence). — [Osasona 2023](https://buildingsandcities.org/insights/commentaries/sustainable-homes-nigeria.html)
- An essay argues for reintroducing the courtyard into contemporary Nigerian architecture. — [Medium, "REANIMATION — a case for the courtyard in Nigerian contemporary architecture"](https://medium.com/@LaoluRG/reanimation-a-case-for-the-courtyard-in-nigerian-contemporary-architecture-1-76220aa9719b)
- Modern duplexes are described as favouring open floor plans, large windows and minimalist exteriors (snippet; low-quality SEO source). — [marketmuscles.com duplex guide](https://update.marketmuscles.com/?p=5519)

### Inferences
- The engine's "contemporary" Nigerian style (flat/parapet roof, boxy massing) matches the market but also the criticism; offering deep overhangs, cross-ventilation and a courtyard/veranda option is a defensible differentiator.

### Gaps
- No citable source on "Lekki style", Mediterranean or colonial style market shares, or wasted-corridor criticism specifically.

## 5. Cultural factors (extended family, hosting, religion, security, power)

### Takeaway
Hosting guests and privacy drive the visitors'/family split and guest rooms; security drives perimeter walls and BQ staff; unreliable utilities drive boreholes (and, informally, generators/inverters). Prayer rooms were notably less preferred in the one Abuja survey found.

### Cited Findings
- Prayer rooms were among the less-preferred optional spaces among Abuja civil servants; ante rooms and visitors' toilets (hosting-related) were strongly preferred. — [Akewusola & Firdaus 2026](https://journals.uniosun.edu.ng/ujees/article/view/492)
- Lack of a dedicated guest room harms family privacy (Ibadan) (abstract snippet). — [Amao & Ilesanmi 2022, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/d60f184b4c854a61871cd197fbec0e5f)
- Boreholes are a necessity where water supply is unreliable (snippet). — [The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2824594)
- BQ (staff quarters) is a common component, usually a separate building at the back. — [Nigeria Property Centre](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/glossary/duplex)

### Inferences
- Default extras for Nigeria: BQ, visitors' WC, guest room, store, borehole + overhead tank position, generator/inverter space, perimeter fence + gate with space for a gate/security post. The last three are inferred from common practice, not cited.

### Gaps
- No citable source for generator house/inverter room/solar room conventions, gatehouse sizing, or extended-family occupancy numbers in modern middle-income homes.

## 6. Global comparison: how other major markets organise the home

### Takeaway
Markets differ most on (a) whether there is a formal guest zone separate from the family zone (strong in the Gulf and Nigeria, weak in the US/Australia/UK where an open great room dominates), (b) whether the kitchen is open (US, Australia, Japan LDK) or closed/service (China, Mexico, Gulf "dirty kitchen"), (c) live-in staff rooms (Gulf, Mexico, Nigeria BQ; declining in Brazil; absent in the US/UK), and (d) orientation/ritual rules (vastu in India, south-facing in China).

### Cited Findings

United States
- Median new single-family home 2,150 sq ft in 2024 (down from 2,200 in 2023, lowest in ~15 years). — [NAHB press release, Feb 2025](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2025/02/affordability-headwinds-driving-home-buyers-interest-in-smaller-more-personalized-homes-in-2025)
- Census 2024: median completed new single-family home 2,146 sq ft; median sold 2,210 sq ft. — [US Census, Characteristics of New Housing highlights](https://www.census.gov/construction/chars/highlights.html)
- Builders' most-likely 2024 feature was a great room (kitchen/family/living combined) (snippet). — [NAHB, top design trends for 2024](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2024/02/top-design-trends-for-2024)
- US kitchens are large, central to family life, without doors and contiguous with living/eating spaces; US middle-class houses lack maid's quarters; bathrooms are large. — [Archinect, Alec Perkins, "Casa vs House", 2013](https://archinect.com/tacos/casa-vs-house-differences-in-us-and-mex-domestic-architecture)

UK / Europe
- Average UK new-build floor space 76 m² vs 137 m² in Denmark (older Cambridge study; ranking now considered dated). — [Ideal Home](https://www.idealhome.co.uk/news/it-s-a-tight-squeeze-brits-are-living-in-the-smallest-homes-in-europe-37458); [Designing Buildings talk page caveat](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Talk:Minimum_space_standards)
- Nationally Described Space Standard (England, Oct 2015): minimum 37 m² for any new home; 93 m² gross internal area cited for a three-bedroom house. — [Designing Buildings, minimum space standards](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Minimum_space_standards)
- RIBA (2015): over 50% of new UK family houses too small; some too small for families to eat together or store a vacuum cleaner. — [Dezeen, 3 Dec 2015](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/12/03/new-build-homes-uk-too-small-riba-architecture/)

Gulf states
- Dual-entrance villa layout: formal entrance to salon/majlis, side or rear door to family living; staff and deliveries use a side/back entrance; central courtyard shields the interior from street view; upper floor often 3–5 bedrooms; closed kitchens around a central foyer in square layouts; prayer room as a flexible conversion of storage. — [Algedra, top villa layouts in GCC](https://algedra.ae/blog/top-villa-layouts-in-all-gcc-countries)
- Saudi villa listing: men's majlis, women's majlis, reception area (maqlat), hall, kitchen, master bedroom (snippet). — [Aqar listing, Khamis Mushait](https://sa.aqar.fm/en/all/khamis-mushait/55?redirected=true)
- Luxury villa CAD plan: separate men's and women's majlis on the ground floor, central courtyard, dirty kitchen and maid's quarters on the roof level (snippet). — [DesignsCAD, dual reception halls plan](https://designscad.com/downloads/multi-floor-luxury-villa-plan-with-dual-reception-halls-and-courtyards/)
- UAE villa plan: main entrance to formal majlis, rear entrance to kitchen and service zones (snippet). — [DesignsCAD, UAE villa ground floor](https://designscad.com/downloads/uae-residential-villa-ground-floor-furniture-layout-plan-1100/)
- Saudi developer (ROSHN) villa plans place a maid's room with its own bathroom on the upper floor (snippet; exact plan not verified). — [ROSHN floorplan PDF](https://alb-home.roshn.sa/docs/pdf/373/400/V1_A_Floorplan_91f21a9a20/V1_A_Floorplan_91f21a9a20.pdf)
- Middle East live-in domestic worker rooms: often ≤10 m² (some 5 m²), reached through the kitchen, separate from other bedrooms. — [ArchDaily, Dima Stouhi, 21 Jul 2021](https://www.archdaily.com/965321/from-hidden-backrooms-to-storage-units-foreign-domestic-workers-and-the-evolution-of-their-living-space)

India
- Vastu zoning per popular guides: kitchen in the south-east (fire), north-west as second option; master bedroom south-west; pooja room north-east; avoid kitchen/pooja in the south-west and bedrooms in the north-east; zoning shifts with plot/entrance orientation (blog sources, not authoritative). — [Housing.com, 800 sq ft vastu plans](https://housing.com/news/800-sq-ft-house-plans-with-vastu); [Design Cafe, south-facing 30×40 vastu](https://www.designcafe.com/blog/home-interiors/south-facing-house-vastu-plan-30x40/); [Asian Paints Beautiful Homes, pooja room vastu](https://www.beautifulhomes.asianpaints.com/interior-design-services/blogs/pooja-room-vastu-tips-for-all-directions-in-house-plan.html); [NoBroker, home plan as per vastu](https://www.nobroker.in/blog/home-plan-as-per-vastu-shastra)
- 30×40 ft is a commonly marketed Indian plot size (inferred from the many "30×40 vastu plan" guides). — [Design Cafe, west-facing 30×40](https://www.designcafe.com/blog/home-interiors/west-facing-house-vastu-plan-30x40/)

China / East Asia
- Beijing resale study (~63,000 transactions, 2019): south-facing apartments sell at a 7.8% premium over west-facing. — [Int. J. Strategic Property Management (Vilnius Tech)](https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/IJSPM/article/view/10430)
- Traditional Chinese homes aligned on a north–south axis; master bedroom and living room face south for sunlight (traditional). — [University of Washington, Visual Sourcebook of Chinese Civilization](https://depts.washington.edu/chinaciv/home/tintrhme.htm)
- Chinese residential kitchens are small and enclosed vs largely open Western kitchens; Chinese design code: ≥4.0 m² kitchen for an apartment with a living room; stir-frying/frying drive fume complaints. — [Purdue (Chen et al.) 2020 paper on Chinese-style residential kitchens](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~yanchen/paper/2020-2.pdf)
- In open kitchens, frying smoke spreads into the living room; semi-open glass-partitioned kitchens evolved as a compromise for Chinese cooking. — [Spacious.hk, open kitchen in Hong Kong](https://www.spacious.hk/en/blog/open-kitchen-in-hong-kong-everything-you-need-to-know)
- Japan: plans labelled nLDK (n bedrooms + combined living/dining/kitchen); a lowered genkan entry for shoes; often one tatami washitsu (4.5 or 6 mats) though increasingly omitted; toilet traditionally separate from the bath. — [Housing Japan, typical home layouts](https://housingjapan.com/blog/typical-home-layouts-differences-between-japan-and-abroad/)

Latin America
- Mexico: full-time maids typical in middle/upper households; small maid's room with a basic shower bathroom expected in a house of any size; kitchens are small, closed "service spaces"; bathrooms small; houses built of block/concrete/brick because wood is expensive. — [Archinect, Perkins 2013](https://archinect.com/tacos/casa-vs-house-differences-in-us-and-mex-domestic-architecture)
- Brazil: the "quartinho de empregada" (maid's room) is described as having its days numbered as live-in domestic work declines, though it persists in new luxury apartments (snippet). — [ArchDaily, "From the Maid's Room to the Outskirts"](https://www.archdaily.com/980816)

Australia
- Average new detached house ~250 m² (HIA; Carlisle cites 250 m² as of March 2024); ACT largest at 258.9 m² (2021-22); all new dwellings ~195 m² (CommSec). — [HIA, why Australian homes are getting smaller](https://hia.com.au/our-industry/housing/in-focus/2025/01/why-australian-homes-are-getting-smaller); [Carlisle Homes](https://www.carlislehomes.com.au/home-files/design/why-your-floorplan-matters-more-than-your-square-metres/); [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/average-floor-area-new-residential-dwellings)
- Common new-home programme: 4 bedrooms, master ensuite, walk-in robes, butler's pantry, home theatre, study/study nook, mudroom, alfresco dining; alfresco and media rooms are post-1980s additions (snippet; feature list from builder pages). — [ArchitectureAU, "The shrinking dream"](https://m.architectureau.com/articles/the-shrinking-dream-household-diversity-and-changing-house-designs/); [Metricon](https://www.metricon.com.au/home-truths/how-many-bedrooms-does-your-new-home-need)

### Inferences
- Butler's pantry (Australia), dirty kitchen (Gulf, Nigeria informally), closed Chinese kitchen and Mexican service kitchen are all versions of the same need: keep smoke, mess and staff work out of the show/social space. A single "service kitchen" option behind a show kitchen would map onto several markets.

### Gaps
- No citable sources for typical Gulf plot sizes, Indian servant rooms, Chinese preference surveys (open vs closed), Latin American plot sizes, or US formal-living-room prevalence.

## 7. Cross-cultural patterns vs regional specifics

### Takeaway
Common across markets: en-suite master bedroom, a service route separate from the guest route where staff exist, and a desire to keep cooking smell/mess away from reception. Regionally specific: gender-separated majlis (Gulf), vastu orientation and pooja room (India), south-facing living rooms and closed kitchens (China), genkan and washitsu (Japan), BQ and visitors' lounge + upstairs family lounge (Nigeria), maid's room by the kitchen (Mexico, Gulf), open great room + alfresco (US, Australia).

### Cited Findings
- Guest vs family separation: Gulf dual entrances and majlis — [Algedra](https://algedra.ae/blog/top-villa-layouts-in-all-gcc-countries); Nigeria visitors' lounge downstairs and family lounge upstairs — [Archinect duplex](https://archinect.com/Olateju-Joshua-Abodunrin/project/4-bedroom-duplex); US great room — [NAHB 2024 trends](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2024/02/top-design-trends-for-2024)
- Staff quarters: Nigeria BQ at rear of compound — [Nigeria Property Centre](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/glossary/duplex); Middle East room off the kitchen — [ArchDaily 2021](https://www.archdaily.com/965321/from-hidden-backrooms-to-storage-units-foreign-domestic-workers-and-the-evolution-of-their-living-space); Mexico maid's room expected — [Archinect 2013](https://archinect.com/tacos/casa-vs-house-differences-in-us-and-mex-domestic-architecture)
- Kitchen enclosure: China small enclosed — [Purdue 2020](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~yanchen/paper/2020-2.pdf); Mexico closed service kitchen vs US open — [Archinect 2013](https://archinect.com/tacos/casa-vs-house-differences-in-us-and-mex-domestic-architecture)
- House size spread: US ~2,150 sq ft (~200 m²) — [NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2025/02/affordability-headwinds-driving-home-buyers-interest-in-smaller-more-personalized-homes-in-2025); Australia ~250 m² — [HIA](https://hia.com.au/our-industry/housing/in-focus/2025/01/why-australian-homes-are-getting-smaller); UK new-build ~76 m² (dated) — [Ideal Home](https://www.idealhome.co.uk/news/it-s-a-tight-squeeze-brits-are-living-in-the-smallest-homes-in-europe-37458)

### Inferences
- A market pack could carry: `guestZone` (none | visitors-lounge | majlis | majlis-gendered), `kitchen` (open | semi-open | closed | show+service), `staffRoom` (none | in-house-off-kitchen | outbuilding/BQ), `orientationRule` (none | vastu | south-facing), `entryType` (foyer | genkan | ante-room), plus default plot sizes. This maps findings to BuildWise's rule that values live in the market pack rather than code.

### Gaps
- No single comparative academic source across all markets was found; the comparison is assembled from per-market sources of uneven quality.
