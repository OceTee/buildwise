# Modern and contemporary residential architecture: principles for a generative house-layout engine (BuildWise, Nigeria / West Africa)

Scope: single-family houses (bungalows, 2–3 storey houses). Units converted to metres where sources use feet (1 ft = 0.305 m). Research date: 2026-11-23. Note on source quality: the strongest quantitative sources found are English space standards (NDSS 2015; GLA *Housing Design Standards LPG*, June 2023, read in full from the PDF) and *A Pattern Language* (Alexander et al., 1977; pattern texts read from the patterns-dev transcription). Popular press (Homebuilding & Renovating, Houzz, blogs) is used only where nothing better was found and is flagged.

## 1. Defining principles of modern / contemporary house design (form and massing, proportion, light, materials, indoor-outdoor, open plan)

### Takeaway
The durable "modern" moves are: a free plan released from load-bearing partitions, long horizontal openings and free facades, outdoor rooms (roof gardens, terraces) tied to the main living spaces, narrow plan depths so every room gets daylight from two sides, and varied ceiling heights; in the tropics these are inseparable from shading (deep eaves, brise-soleil, louvres) and cross-ventilation through shallow, long blocks oriented with closed east/west ends. Contemporary guidance now favours *flexible* open plan (kitchen separable from sitting space) rather than one undifferentiated room.

### Cited Findings
**Canonical modernist principles**
- Le Corbusier's Five Points: pilotis, free plan, free facade, ribbon (horizontal) windows, roof garden; the free plan is usually considered the focal point — without load-bearing partitions the plan gains flexibility. Villa Savoye is the building where all five were combined; its ribbon windows give uniform lighting and a sense of spaciousness. — [Wikipedia: Le Corbusier's Five Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier%27s_Five_Points_of_Architecture); [EBSCO Research Starter on Villa Savoye](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/architecture/le-corbusiers-villa-savoye-exemplifies-functionalist-architecture)
- The points are interdependent: pilotis enable the free facade, the free facade enables the ribbon window, and free plan and roof garden follow from the same column grid. — [Illustrarch: Five points of architecture](https://illustrarch.com/architectural-styles/97480-five-points-of-architecture.html)
- ArchDaily (2020) catalogues the five points in contemporary houses: raising a house on pilotis "frees the ground floor and links public and private space" (Acquired Stilt House / DOG; U-House in Irie / Ushijima); open plans make spaces "more flexible and integrated" (Library House / Atelier Branco; Lilla Rågholmen / Arrhov Frick); non-structural walls free the facade for openings (Badari Residence / Cadence); long horizontal openings add daylight and panoramic views (Villa Oreveien / Lie Øyen); flat roofs add usable living space (Olivos House / Gonzalo Bardach; Torreão House / BLOCO). — [ArchDaily: The 5 points of modern architecture in contemporary projects](https://www.archdaily.com/948273/the-5-points-of-modern-architecture-in-contemporary-projects)

**Plan depth, massing and light (A Pattern Language)**
- *Wings of Light* (107): shape the building as long, narrow wings; each wing "never more than 25 feet wide" (≈7.6 m), a true wing of light ≈25 ft and never wider than 30 ft (≈9.1 m); light from windows falls off sharply beyond about 12–15 ft (≈3.7–4.6 m), so rooms should often be no more than 12 ft (≈3.7 m) deep unless walls are very light or ceilings very high; wider spaces (large halls) can use clerestories; wings combine to form courtyards and a cascade of roofs. The authors' cost analysis (offices) found the narrowest plan cost only ~6% more than the squarest. — [A Pattern Language, 107 Wings of Light](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl107/apl107.htm)
- *Light on Two Sides of Every Room* (159): rooms lit from one side are uncomfortable and tend to go unused (steep light fall-off, glare beside the window); place each room so it has outdoor space on at least two sides with windows in both; a one-side-lit room should be no more than about 8 ft (≈2.4 m) deep (with two or more windows), unless it is tall with white walls and large high windows in deep reveals; a small house gets this naturally with a room in each corner, larger buildings need a "wrinkled or turning edge". — [A Pattern Language, 159](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl159/apl159.htm)
- *Ceiling Height Variety* (190): "vary the ceiling heights continuously throughout the building," especially between rooms that open into each other; low = intimacy, high = formality (figures in section 4). — [A Pattern Language, 190](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl190/apl190.htm)

**Open plan vs defined rooms (current standards)**
- GLA LPG C3.1 (best practice): where open-plan living is proposed, ensure adequate separation between kitchen and sitting space; in homes with 3+ bedrooms show how the space can be modified into two separate living spaces (preferably living room + kitchen/dining), each with an openable window; conversely, a dividing wall provided from the start should be removable without significant structural work. C3.2: avoid internal load-bearing walls; put columns on external or party walls. C3.3: avoid layouts where living space and other habitable rooms are reached only through the kitchen. C3.4: dedicated study in homes with 3+ bedrooms (<7.5 m² so it is not counted as a bedroom). — [GLA Housing Design Standards LPG, June 2023 (copy hosted by Wandsworth)](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- GLA LPG C1.4: identify cultural requirements; examples given include "a preference for the kitchen to be separated from the living and dining spaces" and larger kitchens for specific cooking conventions. — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- Camden planning guidance: permanently separated kitchens and living rooms are preferable; combined kitchen/living is acceptable if floor area is sufficient. — [London Borough of Camden guidance (search result summary)](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/7749954/file/document)

**Aspect, orientation, indoor-outdoor**
- GLA LPG C4.1: new homes should be dual aspect (opening windows on two external walls enabling cross-ventilation across the whole dwelling; bay windows, stepped frontages or shallow recesses do not count; adjacent-side windows count only if at least halfway down the dwelling's depth; aspects at >135° do not count). C4.2: locate main living/eating spaces and main private outdoor space for the best views and orientation, with direct sun plus shading. C4.4: avoid bedrooms/bathrooms on street-facing ground-floor facades. C4.6: avoid large full-height windows to habitable rooms (especially bedrooms) where overlooking or overheating risk is high. C4.7: every habitable room has natural light and an openable window with a view out when seated; C4.8: bathrooms ideally naturally lit with openable windows. — [GLA LPG 2023, C4 and Appendix 3](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- GLA LPG C10: private outdoor space ≥5 m² for 1–2 people plus 1 m² per extra bedspace, min 1.5 m deep and wide (best practice ≥2.5 m); balconies/terraces accessed from the main sitting area or kitchen/dining room; consider shade (adjustable sliding screens). — [GLA LPG 2023, C10](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)

**Tropical modernism (West Africa)**
- Tropical modernism principles as presented by the V&A: solid walls on east and west sides to block the strongest sun, long north and south facades left open to airflow; brise-soleils (semi-permeable screens letting breeze in, deflecting sun); wide eaves against sun and rain; open facades with adjustable louvres and slats for cross-ventilation; passive cooling designed from solar-path and meteorological data; mono-pitch roofs, long low blocks, sculptural screens, with local symbols on facades (e.g. Opoku Ware School screen based on the Ashanti stool crescent). Examples: Wesley Girls School (Fry, Drew & Partners, 1947, brise-soleil wall); Mfantsipim School, Cape Coast (wide eaves); KNUST School of Engineering, Kumasi (James Cubitt & Partners, 1956, horizontal louvres); Scott House, Accra (Kenneth Scott, 1961, vertical louvres). — [V&A: What is Tropical Modernism?](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-tropical-modernism)
- Single-room-deep plans with cross-ventilation and shading pre-dated Fry and Drew in the region by centuries; curators argue African architects (e.g. Theo Clarke) were written out of the story. — [e-flux: contested symbols of coloniality and nation building](https://www.e-flux.com/architecture//552588/contested-symbols-of-coloniality-and-nation-building/) (via search summary); [Artforum review of V&A exhibition, 2024](https://www.artforum.com/events/joseph-masheck-tropical-modernism-victoria-and-albert-museum-2024-557840/)
- Fry & Drew's book *Tropical Architecture in the Humid Zone* (1956) is the classic text (its brise-soleil diagram for Aburi Girls' School is reproduced by the V&A). — [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-tropical-modernism)

**Contemporary Nigerian practice**
- Lantern House, Banana Island, Lagos (Oshinowo Studio / Tosin Oshinowo, 2022): 911 m², 5 bedrooms, 7 bathrooms, three levels (ground/first/second floor plans); described as a "self-contained oasis" in Oshinowo's "afrominimalist" style with "clean, crisp lines", geometric patterning for depth, texture and materiality, and light used to give a warm glow inside and out. — [ArchDaily: The Lantern House / Oshinowo Studio](https://www.archdaily.com/999044/the-lantern-house-cmdesign-atelier); press coverage describes a screened terrace that reinterprets the traditional courtyard as a private outdoor retreat — [CNN Style, 2023](https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/2023/09/07/style/tosin-oshinowo-lantern-house-banana-island-nigeria-spc-intl) (via search summary, not read in full)
- Other Lagos houses on record: Fowóralé House (Oshinowo Studio, 780 m², 2017) — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/947396/foworale-house-cmdesign-atelier); Ishahayi Beach House (Studio Contra, four-bedroom coastal house on a barrier island south of Lagos) — [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/architecture/residential/ishahayi-beach-house-lagos); a Nigerian architect's essay argues for reviving the courtyard in contemporary Nigerian houses — [Medium: "Reanimation — a case for the courtyard in Nigerian contemporary architecture"](https://medium.com/@LaoluRG/reanimation-a-case-for-the-courtyard-in-nigerian-contemporary-architecture-1-76220aa9719b) (title only seen; not read).

### Inferences
- Engine rule candidates (massing/light): cap clear wing depth at ~7.6 m (hard max ~9.1 m) for naturally lit/ventilated houses; this also suits single-banked cross-ventilated tropical plans. Rooms lit from one side only: depth ≤ ~2.4 m per Alexander's strict reading, or relax to ≤ ~3.7–4.6 m with two windows and a tall ceiling — or better, require two external sides for living rooms and master bedrooms.
- Deep, compact square plans (the cheapest-looking massing) contradict both Pattern 107 and tropical-modernist practice; the ~6% cost penalty Alexander quotes (offices) suggests narrow L/U/courtyard massings are worth it. An L or U of narrow wings around a courtyard is the plan type that satisfies "wings of light", "light on two sides", cross-ventilation, and privacy simultaneously.
- Orientation rule: long facades facing north/south, minimise and close east/west glazing; deep eaves or verandas on glazed faces. (In Nigeria, ~4–13°N, the sun is overhead/north-side part of the year, so the north facade also needs shading — inference, not sourced here; see climate research.)
- Open plan: generate living + dining + kitchen as one connected zone but with the kitchen separable (a wall/door position available, its own window), especially as Nigerian households commonly prefer closed kitchens for heavy cooking (the GLA's cultural note supports making this an option, not a rule).
- Every outdoor room (terrace/veranda/balcony) should open off the living or kitchen/dining room and be ≥1.5 m (better ≥2.5 m) in its smaller dimension.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source was found for "modern" massing rules (e.g., number of volumes, offset or cantilever proportions, golden-section proportioning in houses); these remain stylistic conventions in the press rather than citable rules.
- Material honesty (exposed concrete, brick, timber) was not researched with citable sources in this pass.
- Lantern House plan organisation (zoning, entry sequence, courtyard) is not described in ArchDaily's text; only plan images exist.
- Canonical houses (Farnsworth, Case Study House #22, Maison Bordeaux, Geoffrey Bawa's houses, Kéré's residential work) were not researched within the call budget.

## 2. Circulation: avoiding long corridors, and circulation as a share of floor area

### Takeaway
Good houses keep passages short (<~15 m, ideally much shorter), make them room-like (light, a view, a place to sit, storage), and route paths to private rooms alongside, not through, common areas. No authoritative, peer-reviewed figure for house circulation share was found; the commonly repeated practitioner rule is ~10–15% of floor area, with >20% treated as wasteful — treat it as a heuristic, not a standard.

### Cited Findings
- *Short Passages* (132): long sterile corridors "represent bureaucracy and monotony"; evidence points to "some 50 feet" (≈15 m) as the threshold beyond which passages feel dead; keep passages "really short"; make them as much like rooms as possible — generous shape, natural light ideally with windows along a whole wall, bookshelves, seating, interior windows; if longer than ~15 m, break the scale with patches of light or by opening into wider rooms. (Caveat: the transcription notes the 50 ft figure appeared in an earlier version of the pattern.) — [A Pattern Language, 132](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl132/apl132.htm)
- *Intimacy Gradient* (127): sequence spaces from entrance (most public) through progressively more private zones; common areas at the heart, with paths to private rooms passing alongside them; bathrooms halfway between common and private zones; each person has a room of their own. Domestic sequence: gate, outdoor porch, entrance, sitting wall, common space and kitchen, private garden, bed alcoves. Peruvian example: neighbours stop at the threshold, formal friends in the *sala* (front room), relatives in the family room, a few in kitchen/bedrooms; merging the *sala* with the family room "draws complaints" because it breaks the gradient. The same pattern appears in African compounds. — [A Pattern Language, 127](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl127/apl127.htm)
- GLA LPG C2.5 note: the combined living/kitchen/dining area must not include the space immediately inside the front door or any circulation needed to reach other rooms (i.e., through-routes are not counted as living space). C2.8 (best practice): at least two built-in storage cupboards, at least one per floor, and at least 50% of storage located off circulation spaces (halls as useful space). C2.9: a WC on every floor that includes a bedroom. Note 4 to C2.12: homes with 5+ bedspaces and all homes of 2+ storeys provide at least two WCs. — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- The NDSS organises GIAs by number of storeys "to take account of the extra circulation space needed for stairs" (gov.uk summary via search). The difference is quantified in the table: e.g., 3-bed 5-person = 86 m² (1 storey), 93 m² (2 storeys), 99 m² (3 storeys); 4-bed 6-person = 99 / 106 / 112 m² — i.e., ≈+7 m² for the second storey and ≈+6 m² for the third. — [GOV.UK NDSS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/technical-housing-standards-nationally-described-space-standard/technical-housing-standards-nationally-described-space-standard); table as reproduced in [GLA LPG 2023, Table A1.1](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- Practitioner rule of thumb: circulation ≤10–15% of total floor area; AI-drafted plans of ~80–130 m² tend to add too many corridors. — [floorplan-ai docs, Layout guidance](https://floorplan-ai-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/layout-guidance.html) (low-authority source). A small-space design course gives 10–15% of carpet area as a space planners' rule, with >20% a sign circulation can be reclaimed. — [Studio Matrx, Circulation and flow](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/small-space-design/circulation-and-flow) (low authority). A vendor blog claims architects aim for hallways <10% (unsourced). Houzz/GardenWeb case examples range from 32% (924 ft² of circulation) down to 4% of floor area. — [GardenWeb/Houzz: Do You Really Need That Hallway?](https://www.gardenweb.com/ideabooks/17320457/list/do-you-really-need-that-hallway) (via search summary; page blocked)
- Sarah Susanka (*The Not So Big House*, 1998) advises *against* cutting area at the entry and passageways: generous circulation areas make a smaller house feel bigger; she also promotes an acoustically separate "away room". — [Christian Science Monitor, 1998](https://proof.csmonitor.com/1998/0819/081998.feat.feat.2.html) (secondary summary)
- Houseplans.com: where circulation is inevitable (e.g., a stair landing), give it multiple uses. — [Houseplans.com: How to analyze circulation](https://www.houseplans.com/blog/go-with-the-flow-how-to-analyze-the-circulation-patterns)
- Office comparison (not houses): circulation is typically ~26–30% of usable area in offices. — [WBDG / UFC: Circulation – Defining and Planning](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/DOD/UFC/600SERIES/RESOURCES/Circulation_-_Defining_and_Planning.pdf)

### Inferences
- Engine metrics: compute circulation share (corridors + halls + landings + stair footprint, excluding the living/dining zone's own floor) and score it; target ≤12–15% for bungalows, accept up to ~18% for 2–3 storeys (the stair alone costs ~6–7 m² per extra storey per NDSS). Penalise any single corridor run > ~6–8 m in a house (Alexander's 15 m is a building-scale threshold; domestic corridors are far shorter), and require a window or borrowed light on any corridor longer than ~4–5 m. (Thresholds below 15 m are inference, not sourced.)
- Penalise layouts where a bedroom or living room is reached only through the kitchen (GLA C3.3) or where the route to bedrooms crosses the middle of the sitting area (Intimacy Gradient: paths pass *alongside* common areas).
- Encode a privacy gradient: entrance/veranda → reception (*sala*-like formal sitting room, which matches Nigerian practice of a separate visitors' parlour) → family living/dining/kitchen → bedrooms; guest WC near the public end; family bathrooms between zones. Count hall width that holds storage or a seat as "useful" rather than "waste".
- Stair as a feature: place the stair in the living/entry zone, lit, and use landings (study nook, window seat) rather than burying it in a corridor.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or standards-body figure for circulation share in single-family houses was found (RIBA *Case for Space* and the RCA "Dwelling size and usability in London" machine-learning study were located but could not be read: the RCA PDF timed out). This is the biggest quantitative gap.
- Corridor minimum widths for *private* houses were not extracted (the GLA LPG only gives 1.5 m for communal corridors in flats; Approved Document M figures not fetched).

## 3. Entrance sequence, arrival, thresholds and procession

### Takeaway
Respected sources agree that a house needs a transition between street and front door — marked by changes of light, level, surface, direction and especially view — and that interiors gain drama from compression and release: a lower, narrower entry opening into a taller, brighter main room.

### Cited Findings
- *Entrance Transition* (112): entering directly from the street gives no sense of arrival; create a transition space between street and door and route the path through it, marked by changes of light, level, surface, direction and above all view; sound and enclosure (a gateway) reinforce it. Evidence: an exhibit that required crossing a thick orange carpet held visitors longer (Weiss & Bouterline 1962); entrances with more transitions were judged more "houselike", the most houselike approached by a long, open, sheltered gallery with a distant view (Christiano); in Bird's housing study ~90% of residents said their ~20 ft (≈6 m) front gardens were just right or too small though only 15% sat in them — the space establishes a private domain even unused. — [A Pattern Language, 112](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl112/apl112.htm)
- *Intimacy Gradient* (127): the domestic sequence gate → outdoor porch → entrance → sitting wall → common space and kitchen → private garden → bed alcoves (see section 2). — [A Pattern Language, 127](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl127/apl127.htm)
- Compression and expansion: a technique named and popularised by Frank Lloyd Wright (but older), in which a low/narrow space gives way to a larger one. — [Wikipedia: Compression and expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_and_expansion)
- Robie House (Wright, Chicago): the entry ceiling is at most ~7 ft (≈2.1 m), with release at a landing giving long, light-filled views to the living room one side and dining room the other. — [Houseplans.com: Wright's Robie House](https://www.houseplans.com/blog/wrights-robie-house-the-first-modern-family-home); [Archipedia: Frederick C. Robie House](https://archipediavm.upress.virginia.edu/buildings/IL-01-031-0032) (via search summary)
- Fabyan Villa (Wright): a deep, low-ceiling porch leads to a small enclosed entrance before opening into larger light-filled den and dining rooms. — [Preservation Partners of the Fox Valley: Fabyan Villa entrance](https://www.ppfv.org/fabyan-villa-museum-self-guided-tour-entrance)
- GLA LPG B1.1: entrances should be visible and clearly identifiable — canopies or recesses make the main entrance more visible. Best-practice plans list a "generous entrance area". — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- Le Corbusier's *promenade architecturale* (ramp sequence at Villa Savoye) is the modernist counterpart, cited generally in sources on Villa Savoye. — [EBSCO Research Starter](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/architecture/le-corbusiers-villa-savoye-exemplifies-functionalist-architecture) (not read in full; treat as background)

### Inferences
- Engine rules: (1) never place the front door flush on the building line facing the gate in a straight line; require at least one change of direction (a turn of ≥90° or an offset) between gate and door, or a covered porch/veranda ≥ ~1.5–2.4 m deep. (2) Mark the threshold with a level change (one or two steps up, which also helps with flood/rain splash in Nigerian sites) and a canopy. (3) Entry foyer/lobby lower or narrower than the room it opens into; the first view from the door should be through the living space to the garden/courtyard, not into a bedroom corridor or the kitchen. (4) Guest WC and reception room reachable from the foyer without crossing family areas.
- Nigerian compound practice (gatehouse, forecourt with parking, then the house) already provides an outer transition; the engine's front parking court should be treated as part of the sequence, with the door set back from or turned away from the gate axis.

### Gaps
- No quantitative standard for foyer size was found (best-practice plans only say "generous"). The Le Corbusier promenade was not researched beyond general sources.

## 4. Room sizes, proportions and ceiling heights

### Takeaway
English standards give hard minimums (double bedroom 11.5 m² and 2.75 m wide; single 7.5 m² and 2.15 m wide; combined living/kitchen/dining 27–31 m² for 4–6 people; main sitting space ≥3.5 m wide for 3+ people; ceilings ≥2.3 m nationally, ≥2.5 m in London). Good contemporary houses sit well above these: master bedrooms ~16–20 m² plus a ≥1.8 × 1.8 m dressing area, kitchens with island ~20 m², ensuites ~2.9–4.4 m². Ceilings should vary, from ~2.1 m in alcoves/entries to 3.0–3.7 m in main rooms.

### Cited Findings
**Statutory/planning minimums (England)**
- NDSS: single bedroom ≥7.5 m² and ≥2.15 m wide; double/twin ≥11.5 m²; one double ≥2.75 m wide, every other double ≥2.55 m wide; homes with 2+ bedspaces need at least one double; floor-to-ceiling ≥2.3 m for ≥75% of GIA; areas with headroom <1.5 m excluded from GIA; built-in wardrobes count toward bedroom area (area above 0.72 m² in a double / 0.36 m² in a single counts toward storage). — [Designing Buildings Wiki: NDSS](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Nationally_described_space_standard); [GOV.UK NDSS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/technical-housing-standards-nationally-described-space-standard/technical-housing-standards-nationally-described-space-standard)
- NDSS/London Plan minimum GIA (m²) with GLA best-practice figure in brackets, for houses: 2b4p: 70 (77) 1-storey, 79 (86) 2-storey; 3b4p: 74 (84) / 84 (94) / 90 (100) for 1/2/3 storeys; 3b5p: 86 (97) / 93 (104) / 99 (110); 3b6p: 95 (107) / 102 (114) / 108 (120); 4b6p: 99 (111) / 106 (118) / 112 (124); 4b8p: 117 (131) / 124 (138) / 130 (144); 5b8p: 121 (135) / 128 (142) / 134 (148); 6b8p: 125 (139) / 132 (146) / 138 (152). Built-in storage: 2.5 m² (best 3.0) for 3-bed, 3.0 (3.5) for 4-bed, 3.5 (4.0) for 5-bed. GIAs for 2–3 storey dwellings include one bathroom plus one extra WC. — [GLA LPG 2023, Table A1.1](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)
- GLA LPG C2.5: minimum combined living + dining + kitchen: 1p 21 m²; 1b2p 23; 1b3p 25; 2b4p 27; 3b5p 29; 4b6p 31 m² (excluding the area inside the front door and through-circulation). C2.6: main sitting space ≥3.0 m wide for up to two people, ≥3.5 m wide for 3+ bedspaces. C2.3: ceiling ≥2.5 m for ≥75% of GIA (reductions only over kitchens/bathrooms for services). C2.4 (best practice): ground-floor floor-to-floor ≥3.5 m. C2.11 (best practice): a utility room in dwellings with 2+ bedrooms. C2.10: an extra bathroom/shower room in homes for 6+ people. — [GLA LPG 2023](https://wandsworth.gov.uk/media/large/Glassmills/CD5/CD5.28_GLA_LPG_Housing_Design_Standards_(2023).pdf)

**Generous/self-build guidance (UK press; practitioner, not standards)**
- Homebuilding & Renovating (Pete Tonks, updated 28 Nov 2022): large living room up to ~7.6 × 6.1 m (46 m²) for three sofas around a fireplace; kitchen with units on three sides plus island ≈4.8 × 4.2 m (20 m²) — 0.6 m units + 1.2 m passage + 1.2 m island + 1.2 m passage + 0.6 m units; units in 0.6 m / 1.2 m modules; breakfast area ~3 × 3 m for six; breakfast + sitting 4.5 × 4 m minimum up to 5 × 6 m; dining: ≥1 m (ideally 2 m) clear around the table on at least two sides; master bedroom ~4 × 4 m to 4.5 × 4.5 m (16–20 m²) for a king (1.5 × 2 m) or super-king (1.8 × 2 m) bed plus wardrobes; separate dressing room ≥1.8 × 1.8 m; ensuite (shower, WC, basin) ≥1.8 × 1.6 m (2.9 m²), ideally ~2.0 × 2.2 m (4.4 m²); utility ≥1.8 × 2.4 m (4.3 m²), ~2.4 × 3 m (7.2 m²) with boots/coats. — [Homebuilding & Renovating: Room sizes](https://homebuilding.co.uk/room-sizes)
- Susanka: everyday dining table needs at least 3–4 ft (0.9–1.2 m) between table edge and wall. — [Fine Homebuilding: Creating the Not So Big House](https://finehomebuilding.com/2002/02/01/creating-the-not-so-big-house-affordable-comfort) (via search summary)

**Ceiling heights and variety**
- *Ceiling Height Variety* (190): public rooms / large gatherings 10–12 ft (≈3.0–3.7 m); rooms for smaller gatherings 7–9 ft (≈2.1–2.7 m); alcoves or rooms for one or two people 6–7 ft (≈1.8–2.1 m). Traditional Japanese rule: height = 6 ft 3 in + 3.7 in per tatami mat (3-mat room ≈ 7 ft 2 in / 2.18 m; 12-mat ≈ 9 ft 11 in / 3.02 m) — i.e., height grows with floor area. Variation matters more than absolute height. — [A Pattern Language, 190](https://patterns-dev.github.io/patterns/clean2/apl190/apl190.htm)
- Robie House entry ≤ ~7 ft (2.1 m) as the "compression" before the main rooms. — [Houseplans.com](https://www.houseplans.com/blog/wrights-robie-house-the-first-modern-family-home)

**Daylight / glazing ratios**
- US model code (IRC R303-style): habitable rooms need aggregate glazing ≥8% of floor area (Boise handout; Wisconsin uses ≥8% glazed / ≥3.5% openable). Some local codes require 10% (Portsmouth OH, Lowell NC, Columbia MO; openable ≈45% of required glass); skylight-only rooms 15% (Lowell). Portland OR: 6.8% minimum glass. — [Boise IRC R303 handout](https://cityofboise.org/media/3923/433-irc-code-section-r303-light-ventilation.pdf); [Wisconsin SPS 321.06](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/administrativecode/SPS%20321.06(1)(a)); [Lowell NC Ch. 150](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/lowell/latest/lowell_nc/0-0-0-2941); [Portland code 29.30.090](https://www.portland.gov/code/29/30/090) (all via search summary)

### Inferences
- Suggested engine targets for a "good contemporary" Nigerian house (inference from the above, to be put in the market pack's `design` section, not code): master bedroom 16–20 m² with min width ~3.6 m + dressing/walk-in ≥3.2 m² (1.8 × 1.8 m) + ensuite 3–4.5 m²; other doubles ≥11.5 m², width ≥2.75 m (first) / 2.55 m (others); singles ≥7.5 m², width ≥2.15 m; living room width ≥3.5 m (better ≥4 m) with a target proportion between 1:1 and 1:1.6; combined living/dining/kitchen ≥29–31 m² for 5–6 people before adding a separate visitors' parlour; kitchen ~10–20 m² with 1.2 m clear aisles; utility/laundry ≥4.3 m² from 2 bedrooms up.
- Ceiling heights: floor-to-ceiling ≥2.5 m as the floor (GLA), 2.7–3.0 m in living spaces for a hot climate (stratification of warm air), entry/foyer at ~2.4 m to compress, and a double-height or raised (3.6 m+) zone over the main living area or stair as the "release" — consistent with Patterns 190 and the compression-release literature. A ground-floor floor-to-floor of ~3.5 m (GLA best practice) is consistent with these.
- Glazing: minimum 8–10% of floor area per habitable room is a code floor; for daylight and cross-ventilation the engine should aim higher (two windows on two walls) while keeping east/west glazing small and shaded.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian/West African statutory room-size or glazing figure was found in this pass (e.g., Nigerian National Building Code, Lagos State physical planning regulations) — needs separate research.
- No authoritative figures for walk-in wardrobe depth (hanging rail 0.6 m + 0.9–1.0 m walkway is common practice but unsourced here), corridor widths in private houses, or stair dimensions.
- The GLA furniture schedule (Appendix 2) was not extracted.

## 5. Examples of well-regarded contemporary houses and what makes them good

### Takeaway
The reference examples found that can be cited with specifics are the modernist canon (Villa Savoye: all five points; Robie House: compression/release and horizontal spread), West African tropical modernism (Scott House, Accra: vertical louvres; Fry & Drew's shaded, cross-ventilated long blocks), and contemporary Lagos houses (Lantern House, Fowóralé House, Ishahayi Beach House); the strongest transferable lessons are narrow wings, screened courtyards/terraces as private outdoor rooms, and shading devices as the facade's architecture.

### Cited Findings
- Villa Savoye (Le Corbusier, Poissy, 1929–31): the one building combining pilotis, free plan, free facade, ribbon windows and roof garden; ribbon windows for uniform light and spaciousness. — [EBSCO Research Starter](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/architecture/le-corbusiers-villa-savoye-exemplifies-functionalist-architecture); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier%27s_Five_Points_of_Architecture)
- Robie House (Frank Lloyd Wright, Chicago, 1909–10): low (≤~2.1 m) entry releasing at a landing into long, light-filled living and dining rooms on either side. — [Houseplans.com](https://www.houseplans.com/blog/wrights-robie-house-the-first-modern-family-home)
- Contemporary houses illustrating the five points: Acquired Stilt House (DOG; pilotis free the ground), Library House (Atelier Branco; free plan), Villa Oreveien (Lie Øyen; long horizontal openings for daylight and panorama), Olivos House (Gonzalo Bardach; roof garden adds living space), Torreão House (BLOCO). — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/948273/the-5-points-of-modern-architecture-in-contemporary-projects)
- Scott House, Accra (Kenneth Scott, 1961): vertical louvres as primary facade. — [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-tropical-modernism)
- Lantern House, Lagos (Oshinowo Studio, 2022): 911 m², 5 bed / 7 bath, three storeys, "afrominimalist" clean lines, geometric patterning for depth and texture, light as a design material; screened terrace as a reinterpreted private courtyard (press). — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/999044/the-lantern-house-cmdesign-atelier); [CNN Style](https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/2023/09/07/style/tosin-oshinowo-lantern-house-banana-island-nigeria-spc-intl)
- Fowóralé House, Lagos (Oshinowo Studio, 2017, 780 m²). — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/947396/foworale-house-cmdesign-atelier)
- Ishahayi Beach House (Studio Contra): four-bedroom coastal house on a barrier island south of Lagos. — [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/architecture/residential/ishahayi-beach-house-lagos)

### Inferences
- For BuildWise's "concept" and style vocabulary: an "Afro-minimalist / tropical modern" style can be defined by (a) long low blocks with deep eaves or mono-pitch roofs, (b) a screened courtyard or terrace as the heart, (c) perforated/louvred screens (possibly patterned) on east/west and street faces, (d) horizontal openings on shaded north/south faces, and (e) a flat roof terrace where the brief allows — all traceable to the sources above.
- Large Lagos exemplars (780–911 m²) are far above typical BuildWise briefs; the principles, not the sizes, should transfer.

### Gaps
- Plans of the Lagos houses (room adjacency, circulation share, entry sequence) were not analysed — ArchDaily gives only images.
- Widely taught exemplars not researched here: Farnsworth House, Glass House, Case Study Houses, Maison à Bordeaux, Geoffrey Bawa's Number 11 / Lunuganga (tropical courtyard houses), Kéré Architecture houses, Mariam Kamara's housing in Niger, Kunlé Adeyemi (NLÉ). These are strong candidates for a follow-up pass, particularly Bawa (courtyard tropical modernism) and Sahelian/West African courtyard houses.
