# Global house exemplars: what acclaimed modern and contemporary houses have in common

Scope note: 27 houses are covered. 15 are modern classics (1928–1986) and 12 are later work. Of the later 12, 4 date from before 2010 (Latapie 1993, Moriyama 2005, Poli 2005, Palmyra about 2007) but are included as widely published exemplars. The other 8 are 2010–2026. Most detail comes from Wikipedia articles (which cite monographs), ArchEyes, ArchDaily, Dezeen, Wallpaper* and award bodies. Where a claim rests only on a search-result snippet rather than a fetched page, this is flagged.

## Modern classics: what each one is and what makes it work

### Takeaway
Each classic rests on one strong spatial idea that a plan or section can show in a sentence: a raised box with a ramp, a glass slab around a service core, terraces cantilevered from a hearth, an L wrapped around a pool, a wall that hides a garden. Each also has a controlled arrival sequence and a deliberate relationship to one outdoor element (a view, a garden, a pool, a courtyard or a tree). Size is modest: most are 150–500 m² of interior.

### Cited Findings

**Villa Savoye, Poissy, France (Le Corbusier and Jeanneret, 1928–31)**
- Plan: a square on a grid "divided into sixteen equal parts", with the grid reduced from 5 m to 4.75 m to save cost. The plan follows golden-section ratios. — [Wikipedia: Villa Savoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Savoye)
- Section by function:
  - Ground floor: entrance hall, garage, and rooms for the chauffeur and maid.
  - First floor: living rooms, bedrooms, kitchen and an external terrace.
  - Second floor: a solarium.
  — [Wikipedia: Villa Savoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Savoye)
- Entrance sequence: the car drives under the building. The curved ground-floor glazing follows the car's turning circle. Four columns in the hall point visitors to a ramp that is "visible from almost everywhere". The ramp rises to the first-floor living level, continues outside along the roof terrace, and ends at the solarium. — [Wikipedia: Villa Savoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Savoye)
- Facade: the Five Points (pilotis, roof garden, free plan, ribbon windows, free facade). On the short sides the pilotis are set back, which makes the box appear to float. All four sides were designed for view and sun. — [Wikipedia: Villa Savoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Savoye)
- Caveat: the flat roof garden leaked every autumn. — [Wikipedia: Villa Savoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Savoye)

**Farnsworth House, Plano, Illinois, USA (Mies van der Rohe, 1951)**
- Plan:
  - Two offset slabs: the house slab is 24 × 9 m and a smaller terrace slab sits at an intermediate level.
  - Interior is 16.8 × 8.5 m (about 150 m²).
  - Eight steel columns make three bays. The western bay is an open veranda.
  — [Wikipedia: Farnsworth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsworth_House)
- Core: a 4.0 × 7.6 m utility core sits off-centre. Its north face is the kitchen, its south face the fireplace, and its east and west faces the bathrooms. Living, dining and sleeping wrap around it, separated by a movable teak wardrobe. There are no corridors and furniture defines the zones. — [Wikipedia: Farnsworth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsworth_House)
- Entrance: two travertine flights without risers (they "appear to float") climb from ground to terrace to veranda. The entrance is on the west, so the visitor turns 90°. — [Wikipedia: Farnsworth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsworth_House)
- Roof and envelope: the roof slab sits 2.9 m above the floor and cantilevers 1.7 m past the end columns. Glass runs floor to ceiling with no corner mullions. The house is raised above the river floodplain. — [Wikipedia: Farnsworth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsworth_House)

**Fallingwater, Pennsylvania, USA (Frank Lloyd Wright, 1936–39)**
- Area: 495 m² in total, of which 268 m² is indoors and 227 m² is terraces. Outdoor terraces are almost equal to indoor floor area. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)
- Plan: an asymmetrical plan derived from Wright's cruciform Prairie plans, on three storeys. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)
- Compression and release: smaller spaces lead to larger rooms. Hallways are low with windows at their ends. Bedrooms are kept small to push life onto the terraces. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)
- Structure: the terraces cantilever from the stone chimney core. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)
- Materials: local sandstone walls, concrete with streambed aggregate, and walnut joinery. Corner windows have no vertical mullions. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)
- Concept: Wright: "I want you to live with the waterfall, not to look at it." The same materials are used inside and out to blur the boundary, with horizontal stone courses giving a strong horizontal emphasis. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)
- Approach: driveway trellises act as a porte-cochère and the main door is recessed. — [Wikipedia: Fallingwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallingwater)

**Maison de Verre, Paris (Pierre Chareau with Bijvoet, 1928–32)**
- Site: an urban infill built beneath a top-floor tenant who refused to move. The bottom three floors of the old building were demolished. — [Wikipedia: Maison de Verre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_de_Verre)
- Facade: translucent glass-block walls with select clear panes give privacy and daylight on a city street. — [Wikipedia: Maison de Verre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_de_Verre)
- Interior: a double-height main living room. Rooms are divided by sliding, folding and rotating screens, and a rotating screen hides the private stair from the doctor's patients. Much of the article's detail on these mechanisms is uncited. — [Wikipedia: Maison de Verre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_de_Verre)

**Eames House (Case Study House No. 8), Pacific Palisades, USA (Charles and Ray Eames, 1949)**
- Plan:
  - Two separate rectangular pavilions in a line, house and studio, with a courtyard between them.
  - About 230 m² in total: 140 m² house and 93 m² studio.
  - Bays are about 2.3 m on the long sides and 6.1 m deep.
  — [Wikipedia: Eames House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eames_House)
- Section: the 7.0 × 6.1 m living room is double-height with a one-storey alcove, which adapts Wright's compression and release. Only the part beside the courtyard has an upper floor. — [Wikipedia: Eames House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eames_House)
- Site: the pavilions sit against a hillside on an 8 ft (2.4 m) retaining wall. They face a meadow and a row of preserved 1880s eucalyptus. The plan was revised to keep the meadow open. — [Wikipedia: Eames House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eames_House)
- Facade: a black steel grid infilled with glass and coloured panels (red, blue, black, white, beige). The concept is "maximum volume from minimum materials" and "a limited kit of parts". — [Wikipedia: Eames House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eames_House)

**Villa Mairea, Noormarkku, Finland (Alvar and Aino Aalto, 1939)**
- Plan: a modified L-plan. A semi-private garden lies inside the angle, with the lawn and a curved pool, and a more formal edge faces the road. A sauna sits against a low L-shaped stone wall. — [Wikipedia: Villa Mairea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mairea)
- Entrance: a formal canopy leads into a small lobby, then down four steps into an open hall. A screen of wooden poles makes an informal ante-room between living and dining. — [Wikipedia: Villa Mairea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mairea)
- Plan proportions: reception rooms form one "14 metre-square" space. The dining room is a double square and the service block a triple square. — [Wikipedia: Villa Mairea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mairea)
- Roofs and upper floor: the dining room's flat roof extends to a covered terrace that links to the sauna. Upstairs, the children's rooms have angled bays facing the approach, and the guest rooms line a single-loaded corridor facing away from the family garden. — [Wikipedia: Villa Mairea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mairea)
- Materials: wood, stone, brick, rattan-bound columns and white plaster. — [Wikipedia: Villa Mairea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mairea)

**Stahl House (Case Study House No. 22), Los Angeles (Pierre Koenig, 1960)**
- Plan: an L-plan whose two wings wrap the pool, one wing for living, dining and kitchen and the other for bedrooms. "There are no hallways." Every room except the service area faces the pool. — [Wikipedia: Stahl House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stahl_House)
- Size and grid: about 200–210 m² interior on 20 ft (6.1 m) square steel modules. The roof deck is about 370 m², nearly twice the interior, because of the eaves. — [Wikipedia: Stahl House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stahl_House)
- Roof and approach: the eaves cantilever 2.1–2.4 m to shade summer sun and form the carport. Entry is from the carport over concrete slabs that span the pool inlets. — [Wikipedia: Stahl House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stahl_House)
- Facade: the street side is a solid board-clad wall. About 70% of the facade is floor-to-ceiling glass on the view side. Sliding doors cross-ventilate and there was no air conditioning. — [Wikipedia: Stahl House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stahl_House)
- Programme context: the Case Study House programme (1945–66) aimed at inexpensive, efficient model homes. — [Wikipedia: Stahl House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stahl_House)

**Glass House, New Canaan, USA (Philip Johnson, 1949)**
- Plan:
  - A 17 × 9.8 m glass box, 3.2 m high, on a brick floor 25 cm above the ground.
  - The interior is open, zoned by low walnut cabinets.
  - A brick cylinder holding the bathroom is the only full-height element.
  — [Wikipedia: Glass House (New Canaan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_House_(New_Canaan,_Connecticut))
- Site: the house is hidden from the street behind a stone wall at the edge of a crest. Grass and gravel strips lead to it, and tree groups act as outdoor "vestibules". Johnson called the landscape "very expensive wallpaper". A solid brick guest house faces it. — [Wikipedia: Glass House (New Canaan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_House_(New_Canaan,_Connecticut))

**Casa Luis Barragán, Mexico City (Luis Barragán, 1948)**
- Size: 1,161 m² in total, on two floors plus a roof terrace and a private garden. — [Wikipedia: Barragán House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Barrag%C3%A1n_House_and_Studio)
- Street facade: plain, flat, rough cement with only a few small windows and two doors. High walls hide the house. — [Wikipedia: Barragán House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Barrag%C3%A1n_House_and_Studio)
- Entrance sequence: a "bare and dim foyer" acts as a buffer and leads to a high-ceilinged vestibule with "yellow light". — [Wikipedia: Barragán House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Barrag%C3%A1n_House_and_Studio)
- Gardens: the house centres on a walled garden open only to the west. The roof terrace has high walls that frame the sky. — [Wikipedia: Barragán House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Barrag%C3%A1n_House_and_Studio)
- Colour and materials:
  - Colour: strong, deliberately non-harmonic colour on single walls (fuchsia in the vestibule and dining area; red, brown-grey and white on the roof terrace).
  - Materials: volcanic stone, pine and red tile floors, timber beams and thick wood shutters.
  — [Wikipedia: Barragán House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Barrag%C3%A1n_House_and_Studio)
- Concept: an "oasis" shielded from the city. Mexican vernacular masonry with a strong public/private split. — [Wikipedia: Barragán House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Barrag%C3%A1n_House_and_Studio)

**Casa de Vidro, São Paulo (Lina Bo Bardi, 1951)**
- Form: a glazed living volume perched on 10 slender columns. It encloses a courtyard with a tree, and the landscape runs under and around the house on a sloped forest site. This rests on search snippets attributed to Phaidon and others. — [Phaidon](https://staging2.phaidon.com/agenda/architecture/articles/2017/march/08/when-lina-bo-bardi-built-her-own-home); [Arquitectura Viva](https://arquitecturaviva.com/works/casa-de-vidrio-3)
- Planting: Bo Bardi planted the trees that now surround the house. — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/335348/lina-bo-bardis-glass-house-and-the-multiple-worlds-it-contains/)

**Casa Butantã (Mendes da Rocha's own house), São Paulo (Paulo Mendes da Rocha, 1964–67)**
- Size: about 200 m². — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/mendes-da-rocha-house-a-brutalist-manifesto-in-sao-paulo/)
- Plan: all family life sits on "a single exposed concrete platform" raised on a few pillars. Inner bedrooms are lit from above by skylights, and movable furniture rather than fixed walls defines sleeping areas. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/mendes-da-rocha-house-a-brutalist-manifesto-in-sao-paulo/)
- Circulation and garden: circulation is "fluid and informal" and living spaces open to the garden. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/mendes-da-rocha-house-a-brutalist-manifesto-in-sao-paulo/)
- Materials: board-formed concrete. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/mendes-da-rocha-house-a-brutalist-manifesto-in-sao-paulo/)
- Date: given as 1964 by ArchEyes and 1964–67 by a PUC-Rio thesis. — [PUC-Rio thesis](https://www.maxwell.vrac.puc-rio.br/59888/59888.PDF)

**Number 11, 33rd Lane, Colombo, Sri Lanka (Geoffrey Bawa, 1960–98)**
- Origins: four small bungalows on a lane were bought over time and joined. Bawa bought the third in 1959 and the first two seven years later. — [Archnet (search snippet)](https://archnet.org/sites/3008)
- Site and entry: the site is slightly over 6,000 sq ft (about 560 m²). The entry hall contains a two-car garage. — [Archnet (search snippet)](https://archnet.org/sites/3008)
- Plan character: described as "a labyrinth of rooms and garden courts" in which figure-ground and inside-outside hierarchies disappear. — [CEPT student analysis](https://portfolio.cept.ac.in/student/pulkit-jain-ug180472/house-number-11-epitome-of-tropical-modernism-monsoon-2022-ir3611-ug180472); [Socks Studio](https://socks-studio.com/?p=28889)
- Lunuganga: Bawa's country estate (1948–98), an abandoned rubber estate he set out to turn into an "Italian garden from a tropical wilderness". — [Wikipedia: Geoffrey Bawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Bawa)
- Other Bawa work: in the Ena de Silva House "rooms were arranged around an inward-looking central courtyard". Bawa is called "the leader of the Tropical Modernist movement". — [Wikipedia: Geoffrey Bawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Bawa)

**Kamala House, Ahmedabad, India (B.V. Doshi, 1963)**
- Doshi's own house, with an extension in 1986. — [World-Architects / Vastu Shilpa](https://www.world-architects.com/de/vsc-vastu-shilpa-consultants-ahmedabad/project/kamala-house)
- Climate and privacy: daylight is maximised and cavity walls trap and minimise heat. The garden is placed at the rear rather than the front for privacy. — [World-Architects / Vastu Shilpa](https://www.world-architects.com/de/vsc-vastu-shilpa-consultants-ahmedabad/project/kamala-house)

**Magney House, Bingie Point, NSW, Australia (Glenn Murcutt, 1982–84)**
- Plan: a single pavilion divided by a central court into "two self contained suites", one for the parents and one for guests or family. — [Architecture Foundation Australia](https://ozetecture.org/magney-house-bingie-point)
- Section: a repetitive bay structure with large rooms next to a lower circulation zone. — [Architecture Foundation Australia](https://ozetecture.org/magney-house-bingie-point)
- North face: glazed sliding screens with retractable external louvres, under an angled overhang that shades summer sun and admits winter sun. — [Architecture Foundation Australia](https://ozetecture.org/magney-house-bingie-point)
- South face: a closed reverse-brick-veneer lower wall against the cold southerly winds, with high fixed glazing above. — [Architecture Foundation Australia](https://ozetecture.org/magney-house-bingie-point)
- Roof: a thin curved metal roof with an oversized gutter draining to two large downpipes for rainwater collection. — [Architecture Foundation Australia](https://ozetecture.org/magney-house-bingie-point)
- Concept: "tent like qualities", "unexpectedly light, almost transportable". — [Architecture Foundation Australia](https://ozetecture.org/magney-house-bingie-point)

**Row House in Sumiyoshi (Azuma House), Osaka, Japan (Tadao Ando, 1976)**
- Size: site 57.3 m², footprint 33.69 m², floor area 64.7 m², two floors. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/row-house-by-tadao-ando-azuma-house-light-space-concrete/)
- Plan: three equal bays (living, open courtyard, sleeping). Residents "must cross this courtyard under the open sky", and a concrete stair and bridge cross the court. All rooms take light from the court, not the street. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/row-house-by-tadao-ando-azuma-house-light-space-concrete/)
- Street face: a blank exposed-concrete wall with one deep doorway and no street windows. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/row-house-by-tadao-ando-azuma-house-light-space-concrete/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_House_in_Sumiyoshi)

### Inferences
- Circulation is either eliminated or turned into an experience. Farnsworth, Stahl, Glass House and Butantã have no corridors: rooms wrap a core or open off each other. Where movement is emphasised, it becomes an event, as with Savoye's ramp, Sumiyoshi's open-air court and Fallingwater's compression and release. Long, dark, double-loaded corridors appear in none of the classics. Mairea's guest corridor is single-loaded with full-height windows.
- Arrival is staged as a sequence: a shaded or compressed threshold, then a turn, then release into a bright main space. Examples are Savoye's car loop and columns, Farnsworth's floating steps and 90° turn, Barragán's dim foyer to bright vestibule, Mairea's canopy, lobby and steps down, and Fallingwater's trellis and recessed door.
- Outdoor area rivals indoor area: Fallingwater's terraces about equal its interior, and the Stahl roof deck is twice its interior. Classics budget generously for covered and uncovered outdoor rooms.
- Two families of indoor-outdoor relationship recur. One is extroverted transparency facing a view or meadow (Farnsworth, Glass House, Stahl, Eames). The other is a closed street face with an inward court or garden (Barragán, Sumiyoshi, Bawa, Maison de Verre's translucency). The second suits dense plots and hot or public urban contexts.

### Gaps
- Exact floor areas for Villa Savoye, Villa Mairea, Casa de Vidro and Bawa's No. 11 were not found in the fetched sources.
- The Lunuganga garden and terrace layout was not found in reachable sources. David Robson's *Geoffrey Bawa: The Complete Works* is the cited authority.
- The Kamala House vault and plan details were not found.
- The Barragán "cruciform window" was not supported by the fetched source.
- One academic snippet claimed the Butantã slab is "about 3 cm deep". This is implausible and was excluded.

## Contemporary houses (roughly 1990–2026) that are widely published or awarded

### Takeaway
Recent acclaimed houses keep the classics' single-idea clarity but apply it to tight plots, climate and budget. Recurring examples:
- Split the house into several boxes with gardens between them (Moriyama, House for Trees, Palmyra).
- Wrap a cheap, large, climate-buffer volume around a small heated core (Latapie).
- Give rooms movable or rotating parts to respond to the seasons (Sharifi-ha).
- Bury services in a thick wall (Poli).
- Pull one wall or court across the site to organise everything (Casa Wabi).
- Turn an ordinary terrace into an atrium house (RIBA 2023).

### Cited Findings

**Maison Latapie, Floirac, France (Lacaton & Vassal, 1991–93; the architects won the 2021 Pritzker)**
- Form: a simple two-storey volume with a square footprint. An insulated wood volume holds the heated rooms. — [Arquitectura Viva](https://arquitecturaviva.com/works/casa-latapie-8)
- Greenhouse: an east-facing greenhouse half catches morning sun and is "at once interior and garden – open in summer, warm in winter". The house is described as a winter house and a summer house. — [Domus](https://www.domusweb.it/en/news/gallery/2025/12/16/lacaton-and-vassal-latapie-house-bordeaux-for-sale.html)
- Size: the budget covered about 80 m², and the architects aimed for at least double that. The finished house is about 1,830 sq ft (about 170 m²). — [Dwell](https://www.dwell.com/article/latapie-house-lacaton-and-vassal-france-real-estate-fc3bfd64-7f303ef4-b3536caa)

**Moriyama House, Tokyo (Ryue Nishizawa / SANAA, 2002–05)**
- Plan: ten white blocks, one to three storeys, on half of a 290 m² plot. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/01/11/ryue-nishizawa-moriyama-house-21-century-buildings/)
- Arrangement: the "ten prismatic volumes" are set at right angles to one another and to the site. — [MoMA Post](https://post.moma.org/?p=199)
- Gardens: scattered volumes form "a set of connected individual gardens". — [Metalocus](https://www.metalocus.es/en/news/moriyama-house)

**Poli House, Coliumo, Chile (Pezo von Ellrichshausen, 2005)**
- Size and material: 180 m², concrete. — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/476/poli-house-pezo-von-ellrichshausen)
- Thick wall: an oversized, hollow perimeter band holds the kitchen, vertical circulation, bathrooms, closets and interior balconies. This frees a clear central volume. — [Archiweb](https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/dum-poli); [MCHAP](https://www.mchap.co/mchap-emerge-2014-projects/poli-house-)
- Shutters: sliding wood panels hide the services and act as security shutters over the windows. — [Archiweb](https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/dum-poli)
- Use: a cliff-top summer house and cultural centre. — [Archiweb](https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/dum-poli)
- These Poli details rest on search snippets, not fetched pages.

**Palmyra House, Nandgaon near Mumbai, India (Studio Mumbai, about 2007)**
- Plan: two timber-louvred volumes in a working coconut grove. The north volume holds living, study and master bedroom; the south volume holds kitchen, dining and guest rooms. — [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/8735-palmyra-house); [AKDN](https://the.akdn/en/how-we-work/our-agencies/aga-khan-trust-culture/akaa/palmyra-house)
- Louvres: hand-made from palmyra palm trunks. — [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/8735-palmyra-house)
- Size: about 277 m² (about 3,000 sq ft). — [AKDN](https://the.akdn/en/how-we-work/our-agencies/aga-khan-trust-culture/akaa/palmyra-house)
- These details rest on search snippets.

**House NA, Tokyo (Sou Fujimoto, 2010–11)**
- About 84–85 m² made of 21 floor plates at different heights, "like living in a tree". — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/sou-fujimoto-house-na/); [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/230533/house-na-sou-fujimoto-architects)
- Plate sizes are given as 2–8 m² by one source and 21–81 sq ft by another. — [Habitatio BME](https://habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu/en/portfolio/sou-fujimoto-house-na/)
- The completion year is given as 2010 or 2012 depending on the source.

**House for Trees, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam (Vo Trong Nghia Architects, 2014)**
- Plan: five concrete boxes act as giant pots with trees on their roofs. They are positioned "to create a central courtyard and small gardens in between". Glass doors open onto the court, and the outer sides stay closed for privacy. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/06/19/house-for-trees-vietnam-vo-trong-nghia-architects/amp/)
- Walls: concrete cast in bamboo formwork outside and exposed local brick inside, with a ventilated cavity between against heat. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/06/19/house-for-trees-vietnam-vo-trong-nghia-architects/amp/)
- Roofs and cost: the soil roofs act as storm-water basins. The budget was about US$155,000. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/06/19/house-for-trees-vietnam-vo-trong-nghia-architects/amp/)

**Sharifi-ha House, Tehran, Iran (Next Office / Alireza Taghaboni, 2013–14)**
- Boxes: three wood-clad steel boxes rotate 90°. They open onto terraces in summer and close inward in winter. — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/522344/sharifi-ha-house-nextoffice); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/08/22/rotating-rooms-sharifi-ha-house-next-office-tehran-iran/embed/)
- Site and fittings: the lot is narrow, with little side light. The terraces needed foldable balustrades. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/08/22/rotating-rooms-sharifi-ha-house-next-office-tehran-iran/embed/)
- Size and date: seven floors, concrete structure. Completion is given as 2013 by Wikipedia and 2014 by others. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharifi-ha_House)

**Casa Wabi, Puerto Escondido, Mexico (Tadao Ando, 2011–14)**
- The wall: a single 312 m long, 3.6 m high concrete wall follows the coast. It separates public programmes (north) from private ones (south). — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2016/01/14/tadao-ando-casa-wabi-artist-retreat-mexico-coast-concrete/)
- Programme: six freestanding residences branch off the wall, which is also the main circulation route. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/?p=1967)
- Roofs: woven palm palapa roofs. — [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/art/bosco-sodi-casa-wabi-foundation-mexico-interview)
- Note: Casa Wabi is an artists' residence, not a single-family house.

### Inferences
- The strongest contemporary theme is fragmentation: one dwelling broken into several volumes with outdoor rooms between them. Examples are Moriyama (10 blocks), House for Trees (5 boxes around a court), Palmyra (2 volumes) and Casa Wabi (pavilions off a wall). This echoes the Eames pavilions, and it gives every room light and air from at least two sides.
- Climate-adaptive skins have replaced pure glass boxes in hot climates: timber louvres (Palmyra), external louvres (Magney), rotating rooms (Sharifi-ha), a greenhouse buffer (Latapie), and soil roofs with a brick cavity (House for Trees).
- Awards increasingly reward ordinary or tight sites handled with one bold move: the Tottenham terrace atrium, the Accra 40 × 80 ft courtyard compound, the Brisbane pool-encircling addition, and Australia's "doing less". This is relevant to Nigerian urban plots, which are also tight.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian or Kenyan house with major international awards was identified. African coverage relies on Ghanaian examples and search snippets.
- Design descriptions were not retrieved for most RIBA winners (Flint House, Red House, House on the Hill, Six Columns).
- No Gulf or Levant house was researched beyond Iran.
- Indian contemporary coverage is limited to Palmyra.
- AIA Housing Award, ArchDaily Building of the Year and WAF house-category winners were not researched individually.
- SANAA, Kéré, Riken Yamamoto and Murcutt each have additional houses that were not covered.

## Cross-cutting patterns: which moves recur, and how they vary by climate and culture

### Takeaway
Ten moves recur across houses judged beautiful:
1. One legible organising idea: a core, a court, a wall, a ramp or a slab.
2. No long internal corridors.
3. A staged arrival from compressed to released space.
4. Every main room relates to one defined outdoor room: a court, a terrace, a pool or a garden.
5. Generous covered outdoor area.
6. Light from at least two sides.
7. A closed public face and an open private face.
8. Strong horizontal roof lines with deep overhangs, or a single expressive roof.
9. A small palette of honest materials, often local.
10. A modular structural grid.

By climate:
- Hot-humid houses fragment into shaded volumes with louvres, cross-ventilation and courts.
- Temperate and cold houses consolidate around a hearth or core and use glass toward the sun.
- Dense-city houses turn inward behind blank street walls.

### Inferences
These are rules a generative engine could encode, derived from the findings above rather than stated by any source.
1. **Pick one organising idea per design and name it.** Options: around a court, an L wrapping a garden, a pavilion around a core, a cluster of blocks, or a wall spine. This feeds `conceptFor()` and the concept "moves".
2. **Penalise corridor length.** Prefer rooms opening from a living hub, a core or a court. Single-loaded corridors with windows are acceptable and double-loaded dark corridors should be penalised. This fits the existing "hub" fallback in `openingsFor`.
3. **Generate an arrival sequence.** Sequence: a covered porch or canopy, then a smaller lobby, then a turn, then the larger main room facing the garden. This matches Pattern 127 and the Mairea, Farnsworth and Barragán houses.
4. **Score each habitable room on outdoor relation and window sides.** Reward a room for having a defined outdoor room (terrace, court, veranda) and windows on two sides (Pattern 159).
5. **Make the public face closed and the private face open.** Put the solid wall, service rooms and small windows to the street, and the large glazing to the court or garden. This also suits Nigerian security norms, which is an inference worth validating locally.
6. **Size covered outdoor area as a ratio of interior.** The classics reach 50–100% (Fallingwater, Stahl). Use deep eaves of about 1.5–2.4 m or verandas in hot climates.
7. **Pick devices by climate:**
   - Hot-humid (Lagos): fragment into shaded volumes, courts and louvres, with cross-ventilation and raised floors.
   - Hot-dry (Kano or Abuja dry season): an inward court, thick or cavity walls and small street openings.
   - Temperate or cold: consolidate around a hearth or core, with glass toward the sun.
8. **Keep a restrained palette:** two or three materials, one accent colour or material, and a modular grid of about 3–6 m bays.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found that measures which of these features drive "beauty" judgements (for example, jury criteria analysis or preference surveys).
- RIBA's published criteria were not retrieved in detail.
- Simon Unwin's *Analysing Architecture* was suggested but not accessed. Its "primitive place" and "architecture of the house" analyses would strengthen the circulation and threshold findings.
- Cultural variation in privacy, such as the gendered or extended-family zoning common in Nigeria and the Gulf, was not researched with sources here.
